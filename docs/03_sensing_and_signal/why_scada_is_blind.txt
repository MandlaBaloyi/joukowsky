WHY CONVENTIONAL SCADA / LOGGERS ARE BLIND TO TRANSIENTS
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

1. Time scales that matter
--------------------------
  Burst / valve front rise time ............ ~1 ms to ~1 s
  Wave travel between sensors (L/a) ........ ~0.1 s to a few s
  Oscillation period (4L/a) ................ ~0.5 s to ~10 s
  Decay of a surge in a distribution net ... ~seconds to ~a minute
  Diurnal demand cycle ..................... 24 h
Conventional monitoring was built for the LAST row only.

2. Sampling arithmetic
----------------------
A logger recording one value (instantaneous or averaged) every T_log:
  - Probability that an instantaneous sample falls inside a surge lasting
    t_event: ~ t_event / T_log.
      t_event = 5 s, T_log = 15 min (900 s): ~0.6%
      t_event = 5 s, T_log = 1 min:          ~8%
  - If the logger AVERAGES over T_log, a +10 bar, 5 s surge adds
    10 * 5/900 ~ 0.06 bar to the 15-minute average - indistinguishable
    from demand noise.
  - Even if captured, one sample gives no peak, no shape, no arrival time.
Conclusion: at conventional logging intervals transients are not
under-sampled; they are invisible. No algorithm applied afterwards can
recover them. The only fix is sensing at the physical time scale.

Some commercial "transient loggers" exist (burst-mode recorders that
switch to high rate when pressure changes fast). They show the problem
is recognised, but typically operate standalone, unsynchronised, and are
downloaded periodically - they record transients; they do not locate or
act on them in real time. [VERIFY current commercial landscape -
08_research backlog item R-7]

3. What resolution is required? (derived, not guessed)
------------------------------------------------------
(a) To characterise the waveform (peak, rise time, period):
    rise times down to ~10 ms, periods ~0.5 s ->
    sampling >= ~100-200 Hz with an anti-alias filter below f_s/2.
(b) To locate by time of arrival:
    location error from timing error: dx = a * dt / 2
      a = 1250 m/s: 1 ms -> 0.6 m ; 10 ms -> 6 m ; 100 ms -> 62 m
      a =  370 m/s: 1 ms -> 0.2 m ; 10 ms -> 1.9 m
    Target: combined timing error ~1 ms  ->  sampling ~1 kHz or better
    (with sub-sample arrival picking), AND clocks synchronised to well
    under 1 ms (time_synchronisation.txt).
(c) What exists in practice: commercial transient loggers sample at
    128 S/s with accurate timing. That meets (a) and, with sub-sample
    onset picking, gets close to (b). The engine is therefore designed
    for >= 100 Hz input as its high-rate tier and gets better with
    faster data if a source provides it. See data_source_requirements.txt
    for the three data tiers (high-rate / 1 s / 1-15 min).
    [Location accuracy vs rate to be confirmed by simulation study V-S1]

4. Data volume (why the engine works on events, not raw streams)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  128 S/s x 16 bit = 256 B/s = ~22 MB/day per sensor; 1 kHz would be
  ~173 MB/day. Vendors typically store high-rate data on the device and
  send event windows plus summaries. The engine must therefore accept:
    - event windows (raw high-rate data around each event),
    - continuous low-rate summaries (e.g. 1 Hz min/mean/max),
    - full raw streams where a source provides them.
  Our detection library is written so that it can run either on our
  servers or next to the sensor on a partner's gateway.
