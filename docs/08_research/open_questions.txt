OPEN QUESTIONS - RESEARCHED ANSWERS

SCOPE DECISION (owner, v0.2): joukowsky is SOFTWARE ONLY. We do not build
or install physical devices. We consume data from sensors, loggers and
SCADA systems made and installed by others, and we build the engine that
turns that data into detection, location, understanding and learning.
Every answer below is written with that in mind.

========================================================================
PROBLEM FRAMING
========================================================================
Q-P1  How often do municipal-side surges reach private sites, and how big
      are they when they arrive?
  ANSWER  No published measurements for South Africa were found. What the
          literature does say:
          - Utility-caused transients (pump shutdowns) are LARGE but occur
            at few times and places; end-user transients (taps, valves,
            appliances) are SMALL but very frequent (Meniconi et al. 2024,
            real Italian network).
          - On a real service line, user devices produced pressure swings
            of -15 m to +65 m on the service line, but only a few metres
            were transmitted into the main (WEL Perugia, 500 Hz field
            study, 2023/2024). Plastic and oversized pipes attenuate waves.
          - SA reporting links load-shedding pump restarts to water hammer
            and bursts (news and industry sources; not measured data).
  NEXT    We cannot measure this ourselves (software only). We answer it
          by SIMULATION: model main -> connection -> meter -> check valve
          / PRV -> site network in our own solver, and compute how much of
          a municipal surge gets through for realistic configurations.
          Confirm later with partner data.

Q-P2  What pipe materials and ages dominate target sites?
  ANSWER  No SA-wide breakdown exists ("the exact makeup of the pipelines
          in the country is not known"). Install eras are known: cast
          iron ~1870-1930, cement-lined CI ~1930-70, asbestos-cement
          ~1950-70, ductile iron from ~1960, PVC from ~1970; much of the
          municipal network dates from the 1950s-60s and AC is still in
          service in many municipalities. Newer private sites are mostly
          uPVC/HDPE.
  NEXT    The engine must support ALL materials and must never trust the
          asset register. Default wave-speed priors are chosen from
          install decade + material when known, with wide uncertainty,
          then learned from data (05_).

Q-P3  Is slow (non-transient) leakage in v1?
  ANSWER  Transient methods catch bursts and hammer; slow background leaks
          need low-rate methods (minimum night flow, flow balance,
          model-based pressure residuals, e.g. BattLeDIM approaches).
          Important finding: most SA monitoring data that already exists
          is LOW-RATE (15 min, some 1 min / 1 s). A software-only product
          that ignores low-rate data ignores most of the data available.
  RECOMMENDATION  v1 = transient core (our differentiator), but design
          the data model for both rates from day one, and make the
          low-rate module the second thing we build. [DECIDE]

Q-P4  First target: private sites only, or municipalities too?
  ANSWER  Software-only changes this question. Our first "sites" are
          wherever high-rate data already exists or will be installed by
          someone else. Software can be validated fully on simulated and
          public data before any customer.
  RECOMMENDATION  Build and prove on simulation + public datasets first;
          keep the target open. [DECIDE later]

========================================================================
PHYSICS & MODELS
========================================================================
Q-M1  Best friction / viscoelastic models per compute?
  ANSWER  Literature consensus: quasi-steady friction under-damps; the
          Brunone/IAB unsteady model gives most of the improvement for
          little cost; for PE/PVC pipes the viscoelastic (Kelvin-Voigt)
          term is essential and often dominates. A 2024 PINN study found
          models with unsteady friction fit water hammer data best.
  DECISION  Implement quasi-steady -> IAB -> 1-3 Kelvin-Voigt elements.
          Calibrate per pipe from data. Convolution friction later if
          needed.

Q-M2  How much free air is in intermittently supplied networks?
  ANSWER  Not quantified for SA. Research confirms that entrapped air
          (especially during refilling in intermittent supply) reduces
          wave speed, causes dispersion and damping, and can produce
          pressures HIGHER than classic water hammer theory predicts.
  DECISION  Treat wave speed as a TIME-VARYING parameter per pipe, not a
          constant. Track it after every event. Detect refilling events
          as their own class.

Q-M3  Differentiable solver: JAX or custom adjoint?
  ANSWER  Open-source reference exists: TSNet (Python transient simulator
          for networks) - useful to cross-check our solver.
  DECISION  Prototype the solver in JAX (fast iteration, gradients for
          free); production core in Rust; hand-written adjoint only if
          profiling shows JAX is the bottleneck. Validate both against
          analytical cases and TSNet.

SENSING / DATA (re-framed for software only)
========================================================================
Q-S1  Minimum sampling rate and clock accuracy?
  ANSWER  The commercial high-rate loggers on the market sample at
          128 samples/s (Badger Meter PIPEMINDER, formerly Syrinix;
          SUEZ Inflowmatix InflowSense) and use accurate timing to
          calculate wave onset times. SA Water (Australia) holds a large
          128 Hz network dataset. So ~100-128 Hz, not 1-2 kHz, is the
          realistic input.
          At 128 Hz one sample = 7.8 ms -> ~4 m of wave travel at
          a = 1000 m/s. With sub-sample onset picking, metre-level to
          few-metre location on a pipe is realistic.
  DECISION  The engine is designed for three data tiers:
            Tier 2: >= 100 Hz, time-synchronised -> detect, classify,
                    LOCATE transients.
            Tier 1: ~1 s loggers (SA tenders already ask for loggers
                    configurable down to 1 s) -> detect large events,
                    rough classification, no precise location.
            Tier 0: 1-15 min SCADA -> find stressed zones, slow leaks,
                    baselines (Meniconi 2024 used 15-min data exactly
                    this way before deploying high-rate sensors).

Q-S4  Can existing site infrastructure provide data?
  ANSWER  Yes, and for a software company this is key: SCADA historians,
          pump controller / VSD logs (exact pump trip times = free
          labelled events), smart meters, the load-shedding schedule
          (known outage times = predicted pump trips).
  DECISION  Build connectors for these; treat every one as a data
          source with a known quality level.

========================================================================
ALGORITHMS
========================================================================
Q-A1  Best detector?
  ANSWER  Real-time burst localisation with change-point detection is an
          active area (e.g. arXiv:2407.09074). No published head-to-head
          on SA-like data.
  DECISION  Implement CUSUM (fast first pass), wavelet, and Bayesian
          online change-point (confidence output); benchmark on
          simulated data and public datasets; pick per data tier.

Q-A2  Do simulation-trained models work on real networks?
  ANSWER  Not directly. A 2025/26 study on transient-based leak detection
          found a strong sim-to-real gap: direct-test accuracy 30%,
          62.5% after transfer learning with real records. Other work
          shows fine-tuning across pipe materials/diameters works well
          (~96%) and domain adaptation helps across regions (+10%).
  DECISION  (1) Physics-based localisation (arrival times on the graph)
          stays primary - it needs no training data. (2) ML classifiers
          use physical features + domain randomisation + real noise
          injected into simulations. (3) Every model is fine-tuned per
          network as real events arrive. (4) The engine abstains when
          unsure.

Q-A3  Sensor placement?
  ANSWER  Iterative/greedy placement with physical simulation shows good
          results with few sensors ("Just one more sensor is enough",
          arXiv:2406.19900).
  DECISION  Build a placement ADVISOR: we tell whoever installs sensors
          where to put them. Pure software; valuable on its own.

Q-A4  Safe proactive probing?
  ANSWER  Deliberate probing needs actuators (hardware) - deferred.
  DECISION  "Opportunistic probing" instead: use routine known events
          (scheduled pump starts, valve operations, load-shedding
          trips) as free probes to calibrate wave speeds and find
          reflections. Pure software.
