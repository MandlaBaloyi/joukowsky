WHY "JOUKOWSKY"
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

The man
-------
Nikolai Yegorovich Zhukovsky (1847-1921; "Joukowsky" is the French
transliteration used in his publications) is best known as a founder of
aerodynamics - the Kutta-Joukowski lift theorem and the Joukowsky airfoil
carry his name. Less well known is that he solved the water hammer problem.

In 1897-1898 Moscow's water supply was suffering unexplained pipe failures.
Joukowsky ran a programme of full-scale experiments on the city's mains -
pipes of several diameters and lengths, one of them several kilometres long
[VERIFY exact dimensions: commonly cited as 2", 4" and 6" pipes, 7620 m and
305 m long] - closing valves at different speeds and measuring the pressure.
His 1898 report (published 1900, translated into English by O. Simin in 1904)
established three things that are still the foundation of the field:

  1. A sudden change in flow velocity produces a pressure change
         dp = rho * a * dV
     which is independent of the pipe length and far larger than any
     steady-state pressure calculation would suggest.

  2. The pressure disturbance travels as a wave at a finite speed a, which
     depends on the elasticity of both the water and the pipe wall.

  3. A valve closure only produces the full pressure rise if it is
     "rapid", i.e. faster than the round-trip time of the wave: Tc < 2L/a.
     Slower closures let the relieving wave return from the far end in time
     to cancel part of the rise.

(Frizell in the US in 1898 and Allievi in Italy from 1902 reached related
results. Allievi built the general theory; the fundamental relationship is
still universally called the Joukowsky equation.)

Why it is the right name for this project
-----------------------------------------
- Joukowsky's work was a response to exactly our problem: a city's water
  network was breaking and nobody could see why. He answered it by
  measuring - by instrumenting the network and reading the physics out of
  the pressure signal. That is what this engine does, with 21st-century
  sensors, clocks and computation.

- The Joukowsky equation is the single most important relation in our
  engine. It is used both forwards (predict how big a surge a valve or pump
  event will create) and backwards (from a measured pressure step, infer
  the change in flow that caused it - e.g. the size of a burst).

- His third point - "a closure is safe only if slower than 2L/a" - becomes
  a design rule for the engine's own actions. When joukowsky recommends or
  commands a shut-off, it must never cause the very hammer it exists to
  prevent.

- The name signals intent: this is a physics-first system. Machine learning
  sits on top of the physics, never in place of it.
