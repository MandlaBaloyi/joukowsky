MISSION AND ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

Mission
-------
Give water networks a nervous system: see every significant hydraulic
transient as it happens, know what caused it, know where it came from, and
act before damage cascades.

Scope for now
-------------
Solve the technical problem first. The first target environments are
commercially-owned internal networks (estates, factories, campuses, hospitals,
mines) connected to South African municipal supply, because:
  - they are small enough (km, not thousands of km) to instrument densely;
  - the owner controls the valves and can act on an alert;
  - they sit at the receiving end of an unstable municipal supply (pressure
    surges on reconnection, pump trips during load-shedding, intermittent
    supply).
The engine must, however, be designed so that nothing in it assumes small
scale. Municipal district metered areas (DMAs) are the long-term target.

Engineering principles
----------------------
P1. Physics first. Every model has a physical interpretation. Learned
    components refine physical models (unknown parameters, residuals,
    classifiers on physical features); they do not replace them. A result
    that violates conservation of mass or momentum is a bug.

P2. Measure what the physics requires, not what is cheap. Transients live
    in the millisecond-to-second range. Sampling rates, clock accuracy and
    sensor bandwidth are derived from wave speeds and pipe lengths (see
    03_sensing_and_signal), not from what existing SCADA does.

P3. Uncertainty is a first-class output. Every detection carries a
    confidence; every location is a probability distribution over the
    network, not a single point. The engine must be allowed to say
    "I don't know" (abstain) rather than guess.

P4. Do no harm. Any action the engine recommends (valve closure, pump
    change) is checked against a transient simulation before it is issued.
    Closure time must exceed 2L/a for the governing pipe, with margin.

P5. Learn continuously, but auditably. Every model update is versioned and
    traceable to the data that caused it. We must be able to answer "why
    did the engine think this was a burst?" months later.

P6. Fail safe at the edge. Networks lose power (load-shedding) and
    connectivity. Edge devices must detect and buffer locally and survive
    outages; the cloud is for fusion and learning, not for first detection.

P7. Validate every claim. Each algorithm has a validation path: analytical
    solution -> simulation -> laboratory rig -> field pilot
    (see 07_validation).

P8. Be honest about the data. Official statistics are cited with source and
    date; derived numbers show their working; unverified numbers are marked.
