FRICTION, DAMPING AND DISPERSION
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

Why it matters
--------------
How fast a wave decays and how much its front smears as it travels decide
(a) how far from a burst a sensor can still detect it, and (b) how
precisely we can pick its arrival time. Both set sensor spacing.

1. Quasi-steady friction
------------------------
Darcy-Weisbach with the instantaneous flow: h_f = f L V|V| / (2 g D).
It is what the basic MOC scheme uses. It is known to UNDER-predict the
damping of transient oscillations - measured pressure oscillations die out
significantly faster than quasi-steady models predict, because the
velocity profile during a transient is not the steady profile (high shear
near the wall).

2. Unsteady friction models
---------------------------
  - Instantaneous-acceleration-based (IAB, Brunone et al. 1991; modified
    by Vitkovsky et al.):
        extra friction ~ k * ( dV/dt + a * sign(V) * |dV/dx| )
    One empirical coefficient k (typically ~0.01-0.1 [VERIFY]). Cheap;
    easy to add to MOC.
  - Convolution-based (Zielke 1968 laminar; Vardy & Brown turbulent):
    friction depends on the history of acceleration through a weighting
    function. More physical; more expensive (approximated efficiently with
    sums of exponentials).
Decision: implement quasi-steady first (reference), then IAB, then
convolution; choose per network by fit to calibration events.

3. Viscoelastic pipe walls (PE, PVC)
------------------------------------
Plastic walls respond with an instantaneous elastic strain plus retarded
creep. Modelled with one or more Kelvin-Voigt elements (creep compliance
J_k, retardation time tau_k) added to the continuity equation (Covas et al.
2004/2005). Effects:
  - strong damping (often dominating friction),
  - dispersion: the front's rise time increases with distance,
  - apparent wave speed depends on frequency.
The Kelvin-Voigt parameters are pipe-specific and must be calibrated from
data - another parameter set for the learning system.

4. Consequences for detection range (to quantify in simulation)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Attenuation sources, roughly in order of importance in a branched network:
  (1) junction splitting (T = 2/3 per identical tee),
  (2) viscoelastic damping in plastic pipes,
  (3) unsteady friction,
  (4) leaks and consumer connections (energy lost through outlets).
A planned simulation study (07_validation, study V-S2): for representative
estate/campus networks, compute the burst-wave amplitude at each candidate
sensor location for bursts of 1, 2, 5 l/s at every pipe; derive the
detection coverage map as a function of sensor count. This feeds sensor
placement optimisation.

Implementation note: study V-S2, first build (2026-09-28)
---------------------------------------------------------
crates/domain/localisation/src/placement/detectability.rs. The first
front of a sudden burst Q, at each logger: h0 = (B/2) Q at the hole, times
the transmission coefficient T of every node on the fastest path (2/3 at
an equal tee, 1 on a straight run, 2 at a dead end, 0 at a reservoir).
A logger feels it at >= 6 noise sigmas (measured: 5 sigma caught and
timed to 2 samples 96% of the time, 6 sigma 100%, 300 trials each;
tests/detection/analytical/sensitivity.rs).
  - Checked by hand on the estate (1 l/s, 150 mm, a = 1000 m/s:
    h0 = 2.884 m; S3 2 h0, S1 2/3 h0, S2 4/3 h0, reservoir 0) and against
    the full MOC simulation (8 mm hole, 1.009 l/s): every logger within
    0.05% (tests/localisation/simulation/detectability.rs).
  - Quasi-steady friction is left out on purpose: it shrinks a front by
    exp(-f V x / (2 D a)), about 3% per km at f = 0.02, V = 0.5 m/s,
    D = 0.15 m, a = 1000 m/s.
  - Estate, 3 loggers, 2 cm noise: the smallest sudden burst locatable
    anywhere is 0.031 l/s (= 12 cm over 4/3 x B/2, exact). Campus (2.3 km,
    6 recommended loggers): 0.04-0.09 l/s per pipe.
These are UPPER BOUNDS on what is felt: plastic-wall damping (item 2
above, milestone S6), unsteady friction (S5) and bursts that open slowly
all make real fronts smaller. The numbers must be re-measured once S6
exists and on field data (rung 4/5).
What this means in kilometres, and how to extend coverage:
10_findings/detection_range_and_scale.txt (junctions limit range in site
networks, FN-007).

Implementation note: plastic walls, S6 first build (2026-09-29)
---------------------------------------------------------------
crates/domain/hydraulics/src/pipe/viscoelastic.rs. A pipe may carry a
ViscoelasticWall: alpha D / e and 1-3 Kelvin-Voigt elements (J_k, tau_k);
its wave speed a is the INSTANTANEOUS one. Each element is stepped exactly
for a head varying linearly over the step; the creep rate is integrated
ALONG each characteristic (trapezoid: foot and arrival - see FN-021 for
why), linear in the new head, and folded into every characteristic (C' and
B'), so junctions, valves and pumps solve unchanged.
Sharp fronts (V-A6, tests/hydraulics/analytical/plastic_front.rs): a front
shrinks as exp(-kappa x), kappa = sum_k a c J_k / (g tau_k) (theory, high-
frequency limit); simulated kappa within 1.4%. With the illustrative PE
wall (a 400 m/s, alpha D/e 16.7, J 1e-10 1/Pa, tau 0.05 s) a front HALVES
EVERY ~104 m - far more than junctions or friction. If real SA plastic
pipes are anything like this, plastic damping sets the detection range in
plastic networks [VERIFY parameters: the most important open number].
Validated (V-A5, tests/hydraulics/analytical/viscoelastic.rs): reservoir -
400 m plastic pipe - valve slammed shut, no friction. Theory (complex
frequency from s^2 (1 + phi(s)) + omega0^2 = 0): the ring slows from 4.0 s
to 4.5 s and dies away at 0.01023 per second. Simulation: frequency right
(phase drift 0.007 rad over 7 periods); decay +12.7%, +6.4%, +3.2% at 100,
200, 400 reaches - FIRST ORDER in the time step (error halves as the grid
doubles, ratio 1.993) - and extrapolated to a zero step 0.010234: 0.04%
from theory - that was the first scheme (first order); with the
trapezoid rule the decay is +0.4% at 100 reaches, +0.1% at 400.
Parameters used in tests are illustrative [VERIFY: Covas et al. 2005 and
field data]. Not yet: plastic damping in the detectability model and the
placement advisor (they still use the lossless front model - upper
bounds); wall data from network files (EPANET has none).
