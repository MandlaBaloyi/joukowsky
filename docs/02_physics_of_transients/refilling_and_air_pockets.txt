REFILLING PIPES AND TRAPPED AIR (R-5, first rung; gap G-14)
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-10-01) - rigid-column model built and checked
against closed forms (V-A16); not yet in the network solver

Why
------------------------------------------------------------------------
After a supply cut (water shedding, a burst repaired, a reservoir run
dry) pipes drain and fill with air. When the supply returns, water races
back in: the column accelerates down the empty pipe, compresses the air
ahead of it, and where the air escapes through an air valve the column
can slam into a closed end or a full section. These refilling surges are
among the most violent transients in South African networks
(01_/the_operating_environment.txt, section 2). Experiments show peak
pressures several times the supply head for some air-valve sizes (Zhou,
Hicks & Steffler, J. Hydraul. Eng. 2002; Liou & Hunt, J. Hydraul. Res.
1996).

1. The rigid-column model (Liou & Hunt 1996; Zhou et al. 2002)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
While a pipe fills, the water column (length x, from the entrance to the
air-water front) moves as one body - the column is short compared with
the distance a pressure wave covers while it moves, so its compressibility
is ignored until it stops against something. With the supply at
piezometric head H, the entrance at elevation 0, the front at elevation
z_f(x), entrance loss K, Darcy f, diameter D, gauge air pressure p_a:

    dx/dt = v
    x dv/dt = g (H - z_f) - p_a/rho - (1 + K) v^2 / 2 - f x v|v| / (2 D)

(the column gains mass from the supply at rest, so the momentum it brings
in and the kinetic head it costs cancel into the (1 + K) v^2/2 term).
The air ahead (volume V = A (L - x), mass m) obeys a polytropic law
p_abs V^n / m^n = const (n = 1 isothermal ... 1.4 adiabatic):

    dp_abs/dt = n p_abs ( (dm/dt)/m - (dV/dt)/V ),   dV/dt = -A v

and leaves (or enters) through an air valve as through an orifice of
area A_o: isentropic compressible flow, choked above p_abs / p_atm =
1.893 (air, gamma = 1.4).

2. Closed forms used to check it
------------------------------------------------------------------------
a. No air in the way (fully vented), flat, frictionless, K = 0, from
   rest at x0:   (x v^2)' = 2 g H   =>   v^2 = 2 g H (1 - x0 / x).
   With friction:  u = x v^2 obeys u' + (f/D) u = 2 g H, so
   v^2 = (2 g H D / f) (1 - exp(-f (x - x0) / D)) / x.
b. Energy, flat, frictionless, K = 0: d(rho A x v^2 / 2)/dt = rho g H A v
   - p_a A v - the supply's work goes into the column's motion and the
   work done on the air. For a sealed pocket (no vent) the work on the
   air is a function of its volume alone,
       W(V) = (p V - p0 V0) / (n - 1) - p_atm (V0 - V),  p = p0 (V0/V)^n
   (n -> 1: p0 V0 ln(V0/V) - p_atm (V0 - V)), so the column STOPS (v = 0,
   the peak pressure) where  W(V) = rho g H A (x - x0)  - a closed form
   for the peak pressure, independent of the integration.
c. The slam: if the air escapes and the column reaches the closed end
   with velocity v, the column stops in the time a wave takes to cross
   it: Joukowsky, dH = a v / g.

3. Numerics
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fourth-order Runge-Kutta on (x, v, p_abs, m), step limited so the air
volume changes by at most 1% per step (the pocket stiffens as it shrinks).

Checked (V-A16, 500 m DN200, 30 m supply, from 50 m of water):
  no air, f = 0:     reaches the end at 23.3 s, 23.0 m/s, slam 2347 m
  no air, f = 0.02:  95.4 s, 3.43 m/s, slam 350 m
  sealed, f = 0:     peak 205.3 m (n = 1.4), 458.0 m (n = 1.0) - 6.8 and
                     15.3 times the supply - matching the energy balance
  air valve sweep, f = 0.02, n = 1.4, Cd 0.6:
      valve   0.5-10 mm   20 mm   30 mm   40 mm   50 mm   100 mm
      worst   33-31 m     169 m   307 m   341 m   347 m   350 m
  The worst case is a valve large enough to let the column run and too
  small to matter at the end (FN-032).

4. Limits
------------------------------------------------------------------------
One pipe, one air pocket ahead of one column, straight profile; the
column's own elasticity only at the final slam; no air entrained in the
water, no stratified (partly full) flow ahead of the front - those need
mixed-flow (Saint-Venant with a Preissmann slot, R-5 next rung) and the
air valve's own dynamics. Not yet coupled to the network solver. The air
valve is an ideal orifice [VERIFY discharge coefficients against maker
data].
