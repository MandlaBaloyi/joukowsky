PRESSURE, LEAKAGE AND FATIGUE: HOW TRANSIENTS BECOME WATER LOSS
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

The causal chain the engine exists to break:
   surges & pressure cycling -> fatigue, joint damage, crack growth
   -> new leaks and bursts -> real losses (NRW)
   -> higher pressure makes existing leaks leak more.

1. Leakage grows with pressure (and faster than an orifice would suggest)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixed round hole (orifice):  Q = Cd A sqrt(2 g h)   -> Q ~ h^0.5
Real leaks have areas that grow with pressure (cracks open, joints flex).

  Power law (FAVAD concept; May 1994, Thornton & Lambert):
      Q1 / Q0 = (h1 / h0)^N1
  N1 ~ 0.5 for rigid round holes; ~1.0 as a typical network average;
  up to ~1.5-2.5 for longitudinal cracks in plastic pipes [VERIFY ranges].

  Modified orifice equation (van Zyl & Cassa 2014 - UCT, South Africa):
      Q = Cd sqrt(2 g) * ( A0 h^0.5 + m h^1.5 )
  where m is the "head-area slope": how much the leak area grows per metre
  of head. Physically grounded: m follows from elastic deformation of the
  leak opening. (van Zyl & Clayton 2007 on the effect of pressure on
  leakage.)

Engine relevance:
  - Estimating leak flow from pressure needs this law, not a fixed orifice.
  - The (A0, m) of an existing leak can in principle be inferred from how
    its outflow varies across day/night pressure - a slow, non-transient
    learning problem.
  - Pressure management is itself a loss-reduction lever; the engine's
    data supports PRV setpoint optimisation later.

2. Fatigue from pressure cycling
--------------------------------
Plastic pipes (uPVC in particular) can fail under cyclic pressure well
below their static rating. Metals fail at corrosion pits and joints under
repeated loading. Every transient is a load cycle.

Method the engine will use:
  - Reconstruct the pressure history at each pipe (measured at sensors;
    simulated between them).
  - Rainflow counting (ASTM E1049) to extract cycles (range, mean).
  - Miner's rule: damage D = sum_i n_i / N_i(range_i, mean_i),
    with N from an S-N curve for the pipe material.
  - Accumulated damage per pipe, per month -> risk score.
S-N data for aged in-service pipes is poorly known; initially this gives a
RELATIVE ranking ("pipe 17 has absorbed 12x more cyclic loading than the
network median"), which is still actionable. [VERIFY available PVC/PE
fatigue design data and standards]

3. Peak and exposure metrics to record per event and per pipe
-------------------------------------------------------------
  - max head / rated pressure       (overpressure ratio)
  - min head / vapour floor         (cavitation proximity)
  - time below 0 bar gauge          (intrusion exposure)
  - rainflow cycle spectrum         (fatigue)
  - number of events per day        (operational stability)
These are the core "health" features for the learning system.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE (2026-10-01): overpressure ratio in alerts
Pipes may carry a pressure rating (hydraulics Pipe::with_rating; pn(bar)
converts a PN class: PN10 = 102.2 m, PN16 = 163.4 m of water). Every
event measures the highest pressure after its front (features low.rs),
and the collapse peak when it is followed up; the alert compares them,
per sensor, with the LOWEST rating among the pipes at the sensor's node:
  "S2 knee: OVER RATING - the surge took the pressure to 148.8 m, 1.46
   times the 102.2 m the pipes there are rated for: inspect them for
   damage; each such event also spends their fatigue life"
and keeps apart pipes that run over their rating every day:
  "S1 pump: OVER RATING IN NORMAL OPERATION - the pressure already stood
   at 118.2 m before the event ..."
(V-S7: the knee station with PN10 pipes flagged at the knee and the pump;
with PN16, 163 m, nothing.) Ratings come from the site's records - EPANET
files carry none. Not yet: the allowable SURGE pressure, which for some
materials and standards exceeds the PN [VERIFY SANS 966 / SANS 4427];
the fatigue count of section 2.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE (2026-10-01): fatigue counting (section 2), first rung
New bounded context crates/domain/health (kernel only):
  rainflow.rs  ASTM E1049-85 three-point rainflow, live (Counter, fed in
               pieces) or whole (rainflow); a hysteresis GATE drops
               reversals below it (logger noise: 2 cm; default 0.5 m).
  damage.rs    Miner's rule on a power-law S-N curve N = N_ref (S_ref/S)^m,
               and EQUIVALENT CYCLES n_eq = sum n (S/S_ref)^m - the relative
               ranking that needs only the exponent m, not S-N data for
               aged pipes (frontier F7).
  spectrum.rs  a running histogram of cycles by range (0.1 m bins): months
               of loading in bounded memory.
The live Monitor counts every sensor's swings (PipelineConfig.fatigue_gate,
fatigue_bin); Monitor::loading() gives each sensor's spectrum - swings
still open counting as half cycles - and loading_ranking(S_ref, m) ranks
the sensors. A gap ends a stretch: a swing across it is unknown, so what
is open then counts as half cycles (P6).
Checked: V-A19 (the standard's worked example exactly; a steady sine;
Miner and n_eq closed forms; live = whole; the gate keeps the swings and
drops 2 cm noise) and V-S8 (a pump trip on a flat 2 km main: live counts
= whole-record counts exactly; logger record 34.4 vs simulation 34.6

pump's discharge ranks above mid-main). Not yet: mean pressure (matters

Do not count fatigue from SIMULATED boiling zones (FN-038).
