HOW AN ALERT IS MADE - ONE BURST, FROM THE LOGGER TO THE ALERT
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-10-01)

A 10 mm hole opens in pipe P4. Three loggers (S1, S2, S3) hear it. This
is what happens, step by step, with the file that does each step.

Step 0  The site is read                     adapters/files/src/site.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A site file names the network (EPANET .inp, read by adapters/files/src/
epanet/) and each logger: the node it sits on, its export file (read by
adapters/files/src/logger/), its measuring range and clock. Out come the
Site (network, sensors, ratings, zone, power records) and each logger's
record: runs of pressure on the logger's own regular clock, in metres,
gaps kept as gaps. Every assumption becomes a note.

Step 1  The data arrives                     application/src/pipeline/replay.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The recordings are fed to the live monitor in pieces, in time order
across loggers - exactly as a site would send them live. (Live, a
gateway would call the same Monitor::ingest.)

Step 2  Is the data continuous?              domain/telemetry/src/assembly.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each logger's pieces are joined into continuous stretches; a missing
piece is a GAP, recorded and never filled (principle P6). A gap ends
everything that needs continuity: detection restarts, a fatigue swing
across it is counted as half, a supply spell open at it is "not known".

Step 3  Did something happen?                domain/detection/src/
------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each logger, a chain of small pieces:
  noise/      how much this logger jitters when nothing happens
              (robust, from sample differences);
  baseline/   the slowly drifting normal level (Brown's smoothing) -
              demand changing over the day is NOT an event;
  change/     CUSUM: adds up evidence of a lasting shift, in units of
              the noise; past the threshold, an event;
  onset/      WHEN exactly the front arrived, to a fraction of a sample
              (step-area method), with its uncertainty - this is what
              location depends on;
  detector/   the whole chain, live, with the rules for after an event
              (the network rings: echoes are not new events).
Out: an event per logger - arrival time +/- uncertainty, direction
(drop/rise), size of the step.
Beside it, at every logger:
  supply.rs   the supply watch: has this pipe stood empty (about 0) for
              a minute? has the water come back? (supply cuts, refills);
  health/     the fatigue counter: every pressure swing, counted
              (rainflow) into the logger's loading.

Step 4  Correct the clocks                   domain/localisation/src/clocks/
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each logger's clock is a little off. Known offsets (from calibration on
known events) are taken out; unknown ones become part of the timing
uncertainty (GNSS 1 us, internet time 40 ms).

Step 5  What does each logger's signal look like?
                                             domain/classification/src/features/
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Around each front: which way it moved, how far (swing), how sharp (10-90%
rise time), whether it stayed (persistence), and how low and high the
pressure went after it (negative pressure, the vapour floor, the peak).

Step 6  Which loggers heard the same event?  application/src/pipeline/fusion.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two loggers' fronts belong to one event only if the time between them is
possible: no more than the fastest wave travel time between them (plus
margin). Events are grouped so.

Step 7  Wait for the others - but not forever
                                             application/src/pipeline/monitor/mod.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before deciding, the monitor waits until every other logger's data has
come in far enough to say whether the wave reached it - but at most 10 s
behind the leading logger (a gateway off in load-shedding is not waited
for; the alert says so, and is REVISED if its data turns up).

Step 8  Assess: name it, place it, size it, grade it
                                             application/src/pipeline/assess.rs
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  NAME     domain/classification/src/rules/ - transparent rules, each
           adding evidence for or against burst / valve closure / pump
           trip / refill (a drop or a rise? sharp or drawn out? was the
           pipe empty before? did the supply just return? was pump power
           lost?). Evidence becomes probabilities; below 70% the class is
           UNKNOWN - the engine abstains rather than guess.
  PLACE    domain/localisation/ - every point of the network (every 2 m
           of every pipe) is scored by how well it explains the arrival
           times, using the travel times along the pipes (graph/), with
           pumps and valves as they stand at that hour; the wave-speed
           doubt (5% unless the site says otherwise) and clock doubt are
           carried in. Out: the most likely point, the probability of
           each pipe, and the smallest set of stretches holding 90%
           (posterior/).
  SIZE     domain/localisation/src/size.rs - from the drops the loggers
           measured, the flow the hole lets out (+/- uncertainty).
  CHECK    loggers that should have heard it but did not - if they were
           watching, it is not corroborated (Review); if they were not
           (calibrating, a gap), the alert says so.
  GRADE    Alarm / Warning / Review / Info; dangers raise it (negative
           pressure, column separation, a peak above the pipe's rating).

Step 9  Send it - and keep watching          application/src/ports/, alert/
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The alert goes to whatever AlertSink the deployment plugs in (today a
collector; later SMS, email, a dashboard). The monitor keeps the event:
  - late loggers revise it (more arrivals, better location);
  - events that took the pressure to zero or below, or on rated pipes,
    are followed for 30 s more: the collapse peak, the highest pressure
    in the ringing - one revision when all are in;
  - a supply cut confirmed later is tied to the drop that began it.
Times are written on the site's clock ("12:15:03 UTC+02:00").

The same steps, separately
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Supply cuts   monitor/supply.rs: loggers whose pipes stood empty within
                15 min of each other = one SUPPLY CUT alert (Warning if
                two or more agree, Review if one), revised on return.
  Slow leaks    domain/balance/: no waves - meter balance of a zone,
                night flows, a CUSUM watch for a lasting rise.
  Fatigue       Monitor::loading(): each logger's swings, ranked by the
                equivalent number of 50 m swings.

How fast: an alert goes out about 3-7 s (of data) after the burst - the
3 s after the front that classification looks at, and the waiting for
the other loggers.
