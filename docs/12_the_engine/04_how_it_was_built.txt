HOW THE ENGINE WAS BUILT
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-10-01)

Who and how
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner set the mission, the principles and every decision (Rust
first, software only, South Africa first, free-first, build the brain
ourselves, no downloads without approval). Claude (Anthropic's AI, in
Claude Code) wrote the code, the tests and most of the docs, in the
owner's repository, commit by commit; the owner pushes to GitHub.

The method, the same for every piece
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Research and write it down first (folders 00-09): the physics, the
     sources, what to build and why.
  2. Build the smallest correct piece, in Rust, with typed units.
  3. Prove it against an EXACT answer before anything else (rung 1 of
     the validation ladder, 07_validation): Joukowsky's jump, a square
     wave's period, a closed-form flow, a standard's worked example.
  4. Then laws that must hold in every run (rung 2: conservation,
     symmetry, steady stays steady, the grid does not change the
     answer).
  5. Then whole scenarios through the whole engine (rung 3: a burst on
     an estate recorded by synthetic loggers with noise and clock
     phase), and an independent method (cross-code).
  6. When a test disagrees: find out WHY before changing anything. If
     the code was wrong, fix it; if the test or the plan was wrong, fix
     that - and write the lesson in 10_findings/findings_log.txt (FN-nnn).
     Keep 10_findings/known_gaps.txt honest.
  7. Commit with the evidence; all tests pass; the code checker (clippy,
     strictest settings) is clean.

What testing caught (a few of the 44 findings)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of these would have reached a real site if the tests had not
caught it:
  FN-033  A refill against trapped air has no front; its later bounce was
          called a BURST at 99.7% - a false alarm on every such refill.
          Fixed with the supply watch.
  FN-035  After a pump trip, the collapse of separated water columns
          drives the pressure to about TWICE what ordinary water-hammer
          theory predicts - a design checked the ordinary way may fail.
  FN-037  Live alerts lost what their later loggers measured (only the
          first logger's record was measured); invisible on whole
          records, found by testing live.
  FN-039  A fading ring after a pump trip closes no fatigue cycle;
          the first live count showed 1.3 swings where there were 34.
  FN-040  The worst pressure comes in the ringing, 20 s after the alert;
          alerts on rated pipes now follow it up.
  FN-041  Two loggers changing together gave three messages for one
          supply cut; now one.
  FN-044  Told wave speeds are exact, the engine claimed "90% sure" and
          was right one time in five when they were 3% off; it now
          assumes 5% doubt, and its 90% claims hold 88-95% of the time.
Several findings corrected the plan itself (e.g. FN-036: column
separation is an attribute of an event, not a class of its own;
FN-034: build the vapour cavity model before the gas one).

What was NOT used
------------------------------------------------------------------------
No outside libraries for physics, signals, statistics or maths: the
solvers, the sparse matrix factorisation, the random numbers, the
complex numbers, the date arithmetic are our own (decision TD-005: own
the brain, borrow only plumbing). No real data yet; nothing downloaded.
