THE ENGINE IN PLAIN WORDS
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-10-01)

1. The problem, and what we do about it (the owner's statement, 2026-10-02)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
South Africa loses close to half its treated water before it is billed,
much of it through leaks and burst pipes [VERIFY: about 47% non-revenue
water, No Drop report - 01_problem_definition/the_scale_of_nrw.txt].
Today a burst is usually found hours or days later, often only when
someone reports it, and by then a lot of water is gone and the damage is
done. Joukowsky is software that changes that. It takes a site's pipe
network, advises where to place pressure loggers, and listens to them 128
times a second. When a pipe bursts or a pressure shock hits, the wave
reaches the loggers in under a second. Within seconds the engine says
what happened, where it is to within metres, how big it is, and how sure
it is, straight into the site's own control room. That saves water,
repair costs and downtime, and it shows which pipes are being worn out by
the shocks before they fail.

The detail behind it:
Water pipes break, and nobody knows for hours or days. Water is lost,
streets flood, sites run dry, and the bills come later. Worse, pipes are
damaged all the time by things nobody sees: sudden pressure shocks
("water hammer") when a pump loses power, a valve slams shut, or the
municipal supply comes back after a cut. Each shock is a hammer blow on
the pipe wall. In South Africa there are more of them than almost
anywhere: load-shedding trips pumps several times a day, municipal
supply is cut and restored, and many pipes are 40 years old.

Today's monitoring (SCADA) reads a pressure every few minutes. A pressure
shock lasts a second or two. So it is simply never seen.

2. What the engine does
------------------------------------------------------------------------
joukowsky is software. It listens to pressure loggers that already exist
(we build no hardware) - loggers that measure about 128 times a second -
and it:

  NOTICES   that something happened, within seconds;
  NAMES     what it was: a burst, a valve closing, a pump losing power, a
            refill after a supply cut, a supply cut itself;
  LOCATES   where it started - on which pipe, how many metres along it,
            and how sure it is ("90% within these 6 m");
  SIZES     a burst: how many litres a second it is losing;
  WARNS     of dangers that come with it: pressure going negative (dirty
            water can be sucked in), water boiling in the pipe (column
            separation - the collapse can double the shock), a pressure
            above what the pipe is rated for;
  COUNTS    the wear: every swing of pressure each pipe takes (fatigue),
            so pipes can be ranked by how hard they are worked;
  SAYS WHY  every alert carries its reasons, in plain words, and the
            engine says "unknown" rather than guess.

It also watches the slow things the fast method cannot see: leaks that
grow quietly (night-flow water balance), tanks running low, supply
failing.

3. How it can do that - the one idea
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A sudden change in a pipe sends a pressure wave along the water at about
1 000 metres a second (the speed depends on the pipe). The wave reaches
each logger at a slightly different moment. From those moments -
measured to a few thousandths of a second - and the map of the pipes,
the engine works out where the wave came from, the way you can tell
where thunder struck from when you hear it. The size of the pressure
drop tells how big the hole is (Joukowsky's equation, after which the
project is named: a sudden change of flow makes a pressure jump of
a x (change of velocity) / g).

The engine also carries a full simulator of the network - it can play
out what a burst, a pump trip or a refill would do, wave by wave. That
simulator is how every part of the engine was tested before any real
data, and later it lets the engine check advice before giving it.

4. What comes out
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Alerts, like this one (from the end-to-end test, V-S11):

  [Alarm] #1 BURST (100% confident) at 2026-09-29 12:15:03.0 UTC+02:00
    where: P4 at 200.1 m from its start; 90% within 2 m of pipe
    size: about 1.55 l/s (+/- 0.16 l/s), from 3 logger(s)
    seen by: S3 south block, S1 gatehouse, S2 north block
    because: pressure DROPPED by 9.02 m: a burst or a pump trip ...
    because: SHARP front (13 ms, 10-90%): a sudden change ...
    because: pump power is monitored and did NOT fail: not a pump trip

Levels: ALARM (a confident, located burst - act now), WARNING (a
damaging event - pump trip, refill, supply cut, column separation, a
peak over the pipe's rating), REVIEW (unsure, or seen by one logger
only), INFO (normal operation, or explained). An alert is REVISED when
late data arrives - another logger's upload, the peak that followed.

Besides alerts: a report per site (`jk replay`) and a web page to look
at the engine working (`jk view`): logger records, maps, where loggers
should be placed, tanks and supply, night flows, pipe fatigue.

5. What makes it different
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Physics first. Every answer comes from the laws of water in pipes,
    each checked against exact textbook solutions; machine learning can
    refine it later, never replace it.
  - Honest. It says how sure it is, and that claim is tested: when it
    says "90% within these metres", the truth is inside about 90% of
    the time (scorecard, FN-044). It says "unknown" rather than guess.
    It never fills gaps in the data, and says when it could not see.
  - Built for South African conditions: load-shedding (pump trips,
    gateways off), supply cuts and refills, altitude (water boils at
    -8.5 m on the Highveld, not -10 m), local times.
  - Software only: it works with loggers a site already has, from the
    files they export.

6. What it is NOT (yet)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
It has not yet run on real recordings - that starts next (06_honest_
limits.txt). It does not control anything: it watches and warns. It
does not yet learn per network (the learning loops come with real data).
It does not catch theft: theft and illegal connections send no wave; at
most the slow water balance shows a loss it cannot tell from a leak. Its
alert takes about 3-7 s, not under one second: the wave arrives at once,
but the engine looks at the 3 s after it and waits for the other
loggers. A burst makes the pressure DROP; a rise is a valve shutting or
supply returning. It does not yet plug into a site's own control room -
that is the integration plan (11_business/integration.txt), built after
the real-data tests.
