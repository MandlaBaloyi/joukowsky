THE FRONTIER: WHAT NO ONE HAS SOLVED, AND WHAT WE MUST INVENT
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

These are the places where libraries, papers and products stop. They are
also where the engine's defensible value lies: anyone can buy a 128 S/s
logger; almost no one can turn its data into a calibrated, located,
explained event on an old, mixed-material, intermittently supplied
network. Each item: WHY UNSOLVED -> OUR APPROACH -> FIRST PROOF.

F1  Localisation on real networks with UNKNOWN wave speeds
    WHY   Arrival-time localisation is solved on a single pipe with known
          a. On a branched network with wave speeds known only to
          +/-20% (and changing with free air and temperature), errors
          compound and the posterior becomes multi-modal.
    OURS  Joint Bayesian posterior over (source location, per-pipe wave
          speeds, clock offsets), using graph travel times, amplitude
          decay through junctions, and later full-waveform likelihoods.
          Opportunistic probes (known pump/valve events) tighten the
          wave-speed priors continuously.
    PROOF Credible-region coverage on simulated networks with deliberately
          wrong priors; then field datasets D2/D4.

F2  A differentiable transient solver in Rust
    WHY   JAX/PyTorch MOC prototypes exist in research; a production,
          deterministic, network-scale MOC with an exact discrete adjoint
          in a compiled language is not publicly available [VERIFY].
    OURS  Hand-derived discrete adjoint of our own MOC scheme (02_ S8),
          verified against finite differences to tight tolerance.
    PROOF Gradient check suite; ITA on synthetic networks recovers known
          leaks and wave speeds.

F3  Transient-capable graph surrogates WITH uncertainty and inversion
    WHY   2024-2026 work: PI-GNNs for steady hydraulics; one STGCN
          water-hammer surrogate (Feb 2026) reproduces pressure
          histories. None gives calibrated uncertainty, generalises to
          unknown wave speeds, or is used inside inversion.
    OURS  Graph model trained on our own solver's output (domain-
          randomised), with physics-residual losses via our adjoint,
          conformal calibration, and use only as a proposal/accelerator
          inside Bayesian inference - never as the final answer.
    PROOF Beats the physics-only localiser on speed at equal coverage.

F4  Sim-to-real transfer for event classification
    WHY   Reported direct sim-to-real accuracy ~30% (08_research Q-A2).
    OURS  Physical features first (rule-based Stage A needs no labels);
          noise and artefact libraries learned from real quiet periods;
          per-network fine-tuning; mandatory abstention.
    PROOF Per-class precision/recall on held-out real networks.

F5  Municipal-to-site surge transmission (08_research Q-P1)
    WHY   No South African measurements exist.
    OURS  Simulation study: main -> connection -> meter -> check valve /
          PRV -> site network, across realistic configurations.
    PROOF A published transmission table; later partner data.

F6  Column separation and air in intermittently supplied networks
    WHY   DVCM/DGCM are validated mostly on lab pipes; network-scale
          refilling with entrained air is poorly modelled, and SA
          networks refill daily in places.
    OURS  DGCM + air-valve models in our solver; wave speed treated as
          time-varying per pipe (Q-M2); refilling as its own event class.
    PROOF Lab datasets (D6), then partner refilling events.

F7  Fatigue of AGED South African pipes
    WHY   S-N data exists for new uPVC/PE (standards SANS 966, SANS ISO
          4427 cover design); not for 40-year-old AC or cast iron under
          surge cycling.
    OURS  Relative damage ranking first (rainflow + Miner), then a
          survival model that learns failure hazard from observed
          loading and repair records.
    PROOF Ranking predicts later failures better than age/material alone.

F8  Wave speed as a pipe-health signal at network scale
    WHY   Demonstrated for individual buried pipes (Alexander et al.
          2023); not as a continuous, fleet-wide monitoring product.
    OURS  Per-pipe wave-speed time series from every opportunistic probe,
          with drift detection separating deterioration from air and
          temperature effects.

F9  Repairing bad clocks in software
    WHY   Many sources will have NTP-grade (10-100 ms) timing, which
          destroys localisation (dx = a dt / 2).
    OURS  Estimate per-sensor clock offset and drift as latent variables
          from events with known sources (time_synchronisation.txt
          item 3).
    PROOF Localisation error before/after repair on degraded data.
    STATUS 2026-09-28: first proof done in simulation - offsets from 3
          known-source events recovered to 0.16 ms; burst error 21 m ->
          0.07 m. Next: clock DRIFT over time (offsets are not constant),
          estimating clocks and wave speeds together, and field data.

F10 Sensor placement under uncertainty for partners
    WHY   Greedy placement is established; placement that is robust to
          uncertain wave speeds and our own data tiers is not.
    OURS  Placement advisor optimising expected credible-region size
          over the wave-speed posterior.

This file grows as we learn. When an item is solved, it moves to the
relevant design doc with its proof, and stays listed here as SOLVED.
