GLOSSARY: THE WORDS WE USE, IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-09-29) - add a word whenever a new one appears

Transient        A sudden change of pressure that travels through the
                 pipes as a wave: a burst, a valve slammed, a pump that
                 stops. Lasts seconds; can damage pipes.
Water hammer     A strong transient, usually from stopping water fast.
                 You hear it as a bang in the pipes.
Joukowsky        The rule that says how big the pressure jump is when
equation         water speed changes suddenly: jump = (wave speed / g) x
                 speed change. 02_/joukowsky_equation.txt.
Wave speed (a)   How fast the pressure wave runs along a pipe: about 1000
                 metres a second in metal or cement pipe, 250-450 in
                 plastic. It is how we turn timing into distance.
Head (H)         Pressure expressed as the height of a water column, in
                 metres. 1 bar is about 10.2 m.
Front            The leading edge of a wave arriving at a logger - the
                 moment the pressure starts to jump.
Arrival time     When a logger felt the front. Differences between
                 loggers' arrival times tell us where the event started.
Logger           A pressure sensor that records readings, e.g. 128 times
                 a second (128 S/s). We do not make loggers; we read them.
Tier 2 / 1 / 0   What the data allows: Tier 2 = fast loggers (see and
                 locate waves); Tier 1 = 1 reading a second (see big
                 events); Tier 0 = SCADA every few minutes (trends only).
Noise, sigma     The small random wobble in every sensor. Sigma is its
                 typical size. We trigger at 6 sigma so noise alone
                 almost never looks like an event.
CUSUM            The detector's "running total of surprise": it adds up
                 how far readings stray from normal and fires when the
                 total gets too big.
Baseline         What the pressure normally is right now; slowly updated.
Ringing / echo   The wave bouncing back and forth in the pipes after an
                 event. Not a new event.
Impairment       A stretch of time when a logger was NOT watching at full
                 sensitivity (calibrating, blind, ringing). Always shown.
Gap              Missing data. Never filled in; silence is not "no event".
MOC              Method of characteristics: how our simulator computes
                 waves in pipes, step by step in time.
Simulation       Computer-made pressure data from the physics, used to
                 test the engine where we know the truth.
Localisation     Finding where on the pipe map an event started.
Posterior,       Instead of one dot on the map, the engine gives how
credible region  likely each spot is, and a "dig here" stretch that holds
                 the true spot with stated probability (e.g. 90%).
Misfit           How badly the arrival times fit one source. Large misfit
                 = two events at once, a bad clock, or a wrong map.
Classification   Saying WHAT happened: burst, valve closure, pump trip,
                 or Unknown (the engine may abstain).
Alert levels     Info / Review / Alarm: how loudly the engine raises it.
Placement        Choosing where loggers should go so bursts are felt and
advisor          located well.
Detectability    Whether a burst of a given size is big enough, when it
                 reaches a logger, to be felt.
Calibration      Measuring a real number (a sensor's noise, a pipe's wave
                 speed, a clock's error) from data. Learning without ML.
Machine learning Learning patterns that have no equation (e.g. which
(ML)             pipes will fail next) from many examples.
AI (language     The assistant that explains alerts in plain words and
models)          turns repair notes into records. Never decides where a
                 burst is.
Validation       The steps every method must pass: exact physics ->
ladder           published benchmarks -> simulation -> real datasets ->
                 live listen-only mode. 07_validation/.
Shadow mode      The engine runs on live data but only listens; its
                 alerts are compared with what really happened.
