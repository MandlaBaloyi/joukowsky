EXTERNAL FACTORS: WEATHER, LIGHTNING, HEAT, TREMORS, VIBRATION, POWER
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-09-29) - opened by FN-012; none tested yet

Why this matters
----------------
The engine watches pressure. The world outside the pipe changes that
pressure (or the SENSOR's reading of it) in ways that are not bursts. For
each factor below: what it does, how it looks in the data, what the
engine does TODAY, and the test that will prove it (V-E ids, to be added
under tests/pipeline/simulation/ or tests/detection/simulation/).

The rule that protects us from most of them: a real hydraulic event is a
WAVE on the PIPES. It reaches loggers in an order and at times that the
pipe graph and the wave speed dictate. Something that reaches one logger
only, or all loggers at once in a way no pipe path explains, is not a
pipe event (fusion consistency + the misfit check in assess.rs).

E1  Lightning / electrical surge on a sensor or its cable
  EFFECT     A spike or jump on ONE channel; not water moving.
  IN DATA    Very short spike returning to baseline, or a step on one
             logger only, often with a data gap (logger reset).
  TODAY      Single-sensor events are rated Review, not Alarm (FN-003);
             gaps are gaps (P6).
  GAP        A spike that returns to baseline within a few samples is
             not a burst front (a burst's step stays); not yet a rule.
  TEST V-E1  Inject a 1-3 sample spike on one logger during quiet flow:
             expect no Alarm; Review at most, reason in words.

E2  Storms -> power cuts (and load-shedding)
  EFFECT     Pumps trip (a REAL transient we want), loggers lose power,
             uploads arrive late, everything restarts at once.
  TODAY      Pump trip is a class; gaps, late sensors and power cuts are
             handled and tested (tests/pipeline/simulation/live.rs).
  TEST       Covered. Add: simultaneous restart surge after a cut.

E3  Rain
  EFFECT     No direct effect on water inside a sealed, pressurised pipe.
             Indirect: (a) demand falls (irrigation stops) -> pressure
             rises slowly; (b) flooded chambers -> sensor failure, noise,
             gaps; (c) soil swelling / shrinking and ground movement ->
             more bursts after heavy rain or drought [VERIFY for SA
             soils]; (d) municipal supply interruptions -> refilling.
  TODAY      (a) the baseline follows slow changes; (b) gaps and noise
             are measured and reported; (c) not used; (d) comes out as
             Unknown (G-14).
  LATER      Rainfall and temperature as FEATURES in the pipe-health
             model (L5); weather data is public [source TBD, approval].

E4  Heat and cold (temperature)
  EFFECT     Wave speed in plastic pipes depends strongly on temperature
             (02_/wave_speed.txt section 4); day/night and summer/winter
             shift it. A wrong wave speed shifts the located position.
  IN DATA    Consistent location bias that changes with season.
  TODAY      Placement allows 10% wave-speed doubt; the locator does not
             learn the speed yet (G-09 L1).
  PLAN       Learn wave speed per pipe from known-source events and keep
             it per season / temperature.
  TEST V-E4  Locate a burst with a +-5%, +-10% wrong wave speed: report
             the position error; check the credible region widens.

E5  Ground vibration: traffic, trains, construction, pumps nearby
  EFFECT     Adds noise to the sensor (mounting, pipe vibration); rarely a
             front.
  TODAY      Noise is measured per sensor; higher noise = lower
             sensitivity, reported, never hidden.
  TEST V-E5  Add band-limited vibration noise; check no false alarms and
             that the reported sensitivity drops accordingly.

E6  Earth tremors (mining-induced in Gauteng [VERIFY frequency], rare
    natural quakes)
  EFFECT     A ground wave reaches all loggers nearly at once (km/s in
             rock); it can shake sensors - and can really break pipes
             (then a genuine burst follows).
  IN DATA    Near-simultaneous disturbance on every logger, timing that
             no pipe path explains.
  TODAY      Untested. The misfit check rejects timings that fit no
             single source, but "all at once" may fit a point in the
             middle of the network - a possible false location.
  TEST V-E6  All loggers disturbed within a few ms: expect "not a pipe
             event" (new rule if needed), and a real burst 2 s later
             still found.

E7  Wind, hail
  EFFECT     None on the water; power and connectivity only (see E2).

E8  People
  Fire brigade opening hydrants (often in storms and fires), contractors
  hitting pipes, operators closing valves fast. These are REAL transients
  - and, when their place and time are known, free calibration events
  (wave speed, clocks). The feedback capture (G-08) should make logging
  them trivial.

Summary: what is covered today
------------------------------
  Covered      power cuts, gaps, late data, single-sensor faults (E2, part
               of E1, E3b, E5 noise measurement)
  Partial      lightning spikes (E1), temperature (E4, doubt only)
  Not yet      tremors (E6), refilling after supply cuts (E3d, G-14),
               weather as a pipe-health feature (E3c)
