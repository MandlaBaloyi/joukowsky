DETECTION RANGE AND SCALE: HOW MANY KILOMETRES CAN WE COVER?
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-09-29) - numbers are upper bounds until G-02

Short answer
------------
The engine has no kilometre limit. What limits coverage is (1) how far a
burst's wave travels before it is too faint to feel, and (2) how many
loggers are spread over the network. The 2.3 km campus is the largest
network we have RUN, not the largest we CAN run: we started small on
purpose (fast tests on a modest laptop; the first clients are sites).

Is 2.3 km small? For the first target - a campus, mall, hospital, office
park or estate - a few km of pipe is the right size (00_/mission: "km,
not thousands of km"). For a municipality it is tiny: a city has
thousands of km [VERIFY per city]. "Designed so nothing assumes small
scale" (CLAUDE.md) means the path to city scale must stay open - below.

1. How big is a burst's wave? (worked numbers)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A sudden burst of flow Q sends a front of height h0 = (B/2) Q both ways
along the pipe, B = a / (g A)   (02_/friction_and_damping.txt, V-S2).

  pipe                        a [m/s]   B [s/m^2]   h0 per 1 l/s
  100 mm, rigid (steel, AC)   1000      12 979      6.49 m
  150 mm, rigid               1000       5 768      2.88 m
  300 mm, rigid               1000       1 442      0.72 m
  150 mm, plastic (uPVC)       350       2 019      1.01 m
  110 mm, plastic (HDPE)       350       3 754      1.88 m

  Working (150 mm rigid): A = pi/4 x 0.15^2 = 0.01767 m^2;
  B = 1000 / (9.81 x 0.01767) = 5768; h0 = 5768 / 2 x 0.001 = 2.88 m.

A logger feels a front at 6 noise sigmas (FN-005). With 2 cm noise that
is 0.12 m. So in 150 mm rigid pipe a 1 l/s burst starts 24x louder than
it needs to be.

2. What shrinks it on the way
------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Junctions. An equal tee passes 2/3 of a front (V-A3). After n tees:
    (2/3)^n. 24x of headroom lasts ln 24 / ln 1.5 = 7.8 -> 7 tees.
      1 l/s: ~7 tees    0.5 l/s: ~6    5 l/s: ~11    0.1 l/s: ~2
    (A dead end doubles a front; a reservoir erases it.)
(b) Friction (quasi-steady): exp(-f V x / (2 D a)) = about 3.3% per km
    at f = 0.02, V = 0.5 m/s, D = 0.15 m, a = 1000 m/s. 24x of headroom
    would last ln 24 / 3.33e-5 per m = ~95 km of straight pipe.
(c) Plastic walls (viscoelastic). NOT MODELLED YET (G-02). Expected to be
    the largest loss in plastic pipe; unknown until S6 and field data.
(d) Unsteady friction (G-03), service connections and leaks: smaller.
(e) Slow openings: a burst that opens over a second makes a gentler front
    than a sudden one: the step reaches about the same height if the
    opening is short compared with the echo times, but it rises slowly,
    so it is harder to time and, if much slower, harder to feel.

Conclusion (FN-007): in a branched site network, JUNCTIONS limit range,
not kilometres. In a long transmission main with few branches, the limit
is plastic damping and friction - potentially many km between loggers,
to be measured on real data. 95 km is a mathematical ceiling, not a
promise.

3. What limits scale in the SOFTWARE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Live engine (detect + locate): work per logger stream plus one
    location search per event over the pipe grid (5 m spacing: 100 km of
    pipe = 20 000 points). Expected cheap; NOT yet timed (G-10).
  MOC simulator: grows with pipe length x simulated time. Not yet timed
    beyond the campus.
  Placement advisor (offline planning): grows with length SQUARED
    (FN-008). Campus 2.3 km ~6 s (release). Scaling estimate:
      10 km    ~19x  -> ~2 min
      50 km   ~470x  -> ~47 min
      100 km ~1900x  -> ~3 h
    Too slow past ~50 km. Fixes, in order of effort: coarser trial bursts
    far from loggers; scoring in parallel (4 cores here, many in the
    cloud); an analytical score for the pair search.

4. How we extend the kilometres
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Zones, like cellphone towers. A city is split into zones (supply
   areas / DMAs), each with its own loggers; the engine runs zones side
   by side. A zone of 5-20 km behaves like a large site. The total length
   is then limited only by computers, not by method.
2. Bigger test networks, without downloading: generate synthetic towns of
   10, 50, 100 km with our own code (grids, branches, loops, mains).
   Time the simulator, live engine and advisor on them (closes G-10).
3. Public networks, WITH owner approval: EPANET example networks, L-Town
   (BattLeDIM) - real layouts, read by our .inp reader.
4. Speed up the advisor (section 3).
5. Model plastic walls (S6, G-02), then re-measure range per material.
6. Use every tier of data together: slow SCADA and 1 s loggers across
   the whole network say WHICH ZONE; fast loggers inside it say WHERE.
7. Mains first: transmission mains have few junctions, so few loggers
   cover many km - the cheapest way to add kilometres.

5. Measured (2026-09-29, `jk scale`, release build, this i3 laptop;
   seconds wander +/-40% with heat - FN-016)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Town (synthetic, crates/sim)   10 km        50 km        100 km
  pipes / junctions              125 / 106    601 / 514    1330 / 1114
  steady state                   3 ms         0.37 s       5.9 s (dense)
  5 s of burst simulated         0.4 s        4.4 s        38 s
  locate one burst (live alert)  2 ms         5 ms (was 66 ms)  230 ms (before)
  score one logger placement     0.3 s        18 s (was 112 s)  - (was: 7 GB, stopped)

What changed (all exact - identical answers, tests prove it): skipping
pipes and 8-cell stretches that cannot hold the burst (posterior/bound.rs),
models built only where needed, one shared bench for every placement the
advisor compares (each trial burst located once per set of loggers that
feel it), four cores, lazy greedy.
Why 50 km planning is still slow: a 50 km town is only ~3 km across and
a 1 l/s burst is felt 1-2 km away, so each new logger touches most of the
town. Next: ZONES (plan each part of town with its neighbours' loggers
fixed) - the cellphone-tower idea of section 4.

Zones (2026-09-29, placement/zones.rs): 50 km town, 50 loggers, 10 zones:
13.5 min, mean 90% region 69 m (evenly spread loggers: 119 m). Cost of
zones measured on a 5 km town: +11% region vs planning it whole (FN-019).

Plastic walls change the range picture (2026-09-29, V-A6): a sharp front in
plastic pipe shrinks as exp(-kappa x), kappa = sum a c J / (g tau). With
illustrative PE figures it halves every ~100 m - stronger than 7 tees. Real
creep figures for SA plastic pipes are now the most important unknown for
range claims [VERIFY]. Planning at 50 km (FN-022): whole town 30.7 min,
56 m - better than zones (60 m); zones only beyond that size.
