KNOWN GAPS: WHAT IS NOT BUILT OR NOT PROVEN YET
STATUS: DRAFT - living list, updated as gaps open and close (2026-09-29)

Read this before telling anyone what the engine can do. Every claim we
make must survive this list. A gap is CLOSED only with a date and the test
or dataset that proves it.

State in one sentence (2026-09-29): the heart is built - detect, classify
(rules), locate, grade, live - and has passed every analytical and
simulation test (194/194), but has not yet been checked against an
independent simulator, does not model plastic pipe walls, and has never
seen real data. Like a team that has won every practice match and has not
yet played a real game.

A. Not yet PROVEN (the engine exists; the evidence does not)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
G-01  No independent cross-check of our simulator          IN PROGRESS
  WHY IT MATTERS  Our MOC simulator generates the data our detector and
    locator are tested on. If both share one wrong assumption, every
    test still passes. An independent code breaks that circle.
  CLOSE BY        Rung 3 cross_code/: run the same networks in TSNet
    (Python, 09_/03) and compare pressure histories. Needs a Python
    install + TSNet download -> owner approval first (TD-020).
    Without installing anything: type in published benchmark cases
    (textbook worked examples, V-A2 Michaud, V-B1 lab numbers from
    papers) as analytical tests.
  AFFECTS         Everything downstream of the simulator.
  2026-10-01: an independent METHOD now agrees with it - the
    frequency-domain transfer-matrix model (S9, no time steps, no grid)
    and the MOC give the branched estate's response to within 0.15% at
    seven frequencies (V-X1). That checks wave travel, reflections and
    junctions. Still open: an independent CODE (TSNet - needs the owner's
    approval to install Python), devices and friction in the frequency
    model, and laboratory data (V-B1).

G-02  Plastic pipe walls are not modelled (viscoelastic damping) IN PROGRESS
  2026-09-29: the wall model is built and validated against exact theory
  (V-A5 ring, V-A6 sharp front), and the detectability model and placement
  advisor now include plastic damping (checked against the simulator
  within 4%), separating the sharp front (timing) from the drop felt
  within 0.25 s (noticing) - FN-023. Two measured walls from the
  literature are in the code (FN-024). Still open: SA pipe classes and
  temperature; timing uncertainty in plastic pipes (the onset picker on a
  blurred front) - the placement still assumes 1 ms; wall data when
  reading network files (EPANET has none).
  WHY IT MATTERS  uPVC and HDPE walls stretch and slowly relax, soaking
    up the wave and smearing its front (02_/friction_and_damping.txt,
    02_/wave_speed.txt). Much newer South African pipe is plastic. Today
    our fronts are UPPER BOUNDS: real ones will be smaller and softer, so
    the detection range and the smallest locatable burst are optimistic
    for plastic networks.
  CLOSE BY        Solver milestone S6 (1-3 Kelvin-Voigt elements for
    PE/PVC, 09_/02), parameters from published lab data [VERIFY];
    then re-run V-S2 detectability and the placement numbers.
  RELATED         S5 unsteady friction (G-03).

G-03  Unsteady friction not modelled (solver S5)                  OPEN
  Quasi-steady friction under-damps waves after the first front. Matters
  for echoes, ringing and later arrivals more than for the first front.

G-04  Never run on real data                                      OPEN
  WHY IT MATTERS  Real noise, real clocks, real sensor faults, real pipe
    physics. Rungs 4 and 5 of the ladder (07_) have not started.
  CLOSE BY        real_data_candidates.txt -> owner chooses -> download
    into data/ (quarantine, manifest, verify) -> datasets/ tests.
  NOTE            The readers are ready (EPANET .inp, logger CSV) and
    were tested on realistic hand-made files; FN-010 shows why this
    matters. 2026-10-01: a whole site can now be given as a site file
    and replayed (`jk replay`, V-S11) - the path real data will take.

G-05  No scorecard over many events                        IN PROGRESS
  Tests check single cases. The metrics in 07_ (detection rate vs size,
  false alarms per sensor-day, location error in m, credible-region
  coverage) need a harness that runs many events and reports them.
  A score needs ground truth: lab data with known burst positions, or a
  partner opening a hydrant at a known place and time.
  2026-10-01: the scorecard runs on simulation (V-S12): detection by
    size, class, location error, coverage of the 90% region, size error,
    false alerts per sensor-day. It found the engine overclaiming when
    told wave speeds are exact (FN-044) - fixed. Open: other networks
    (bigger, looped, plastic pipes), events other than bursts, slower
    loggers, false alerts from real disturbances (pumps, valves, users)
    - and the same ruler on real data.

G-06  Burst size is not in the alert             CLOSED 2026-09-30
  Alert.size from localisation/src/size.rs; pipeline scenario: 1.55 l/s
  estimated for a hole letting out 1.55 l/s (tests/pipeline/simulation/

G-07  No safe action advice (P4)                                  OPEN
  "Close valve V7 over at least 30 s" - simulated first, closure time
  > 2L/a with margin. Needs valve positions in the network model.

G-08  No feedback capture                                         OPEN
  Operators cannot yet confirm/reject an alert or record what the repair
  crew found. These labels are the most valuable data the system will
  ever get (05_/learning_over_time.txt) - learning cannot start without
  them.

G-09  Learning loops mostly not built                             OPEN
  Built: per-sensor noise (L2, at calibration), clock offsets from known-
  source events (L2/F9). Not built: wave speed updating (L1), "normal for
  this site" (L3), classifier Stage B/C (L4), pipe health (L5), across
  sites (L6).

G-10  Large networks                                       IN PROGRESS
  2026-09-29: synthetic towns of 2-100 km (crates/sim) and `jk scale`
  measure every part (FN-013). Live side: fine to 100 km. Planning: much
  faster (exact pruning, shared bench, 4 cores, lazy greedy - FN-014),
  numbers in detection_range_and_scale.txt. 2026-09-30: sparse steady
  solver - 1000 km in 0.29 s (FN-026). Still open: planning hundreds of km
  (zones); the MOC on one core with its time step set by the shortest pipe
  (dt 0.36 ms at 100 km; 4.8 s per simulated second at 150 km on four
  cores since FN-027).

G-16  Lazy greedy assumes diminishing returns                    OPEN
  The advisor re-checks only candidates whose last gain could still win
  (CELF). Not proven for the 90%-region objective; identical to full
  greedy on every test site so far. Check on more networks; the swaps
  repair what greed misses.

G-17  Clock repair: jumps, wandering rates, unknown sources       OPEN
  Offsets and steady drift are estimated from known events (F9). Not yet:
  a resync that jumps the clock mid-record, a rate that wanders with
  temperature, clocks estimated from ordinary (unknown-source) events.

G-11  External factors untested                                   OPEN
  Lightning spikes, tremors, vibration, temperature. external_factors.txt
  lists the tests (V-E1..V-E6).

G-12  Slow leaks are invisible to the transient method     IN PROGRESS
  2026-09-30: water balance of metered zones and a night-flow leak watch
  built and validated in simulation (jk-balance, FN-025). Open: real zone
  data, pressure-dependent leakage, drifting meters.
  A leak that grows over weeks sends no sharp wave. P2: we must not claim
  to see it from fronts. Needs Tier 0 methods (night flow, mass balance,
  pressure trends; V-B2 BattLeDIM; 08_ Q-P3).

G-13  Wave-speed doubt in placement is a fixed 10%                OPEN
  F10 proper: robust placement over a posterior of wave speeds.

G-14  Refilling / air-pocket transients have no class             OPEN
  2026-10-01: the physics has a first rung - one pipe refilling against
  free air, a sealed pocket or an air valve (hydraulics refill.rs, V-A16,
  FN-032). Class REFILL added the same day (V-S6): rise from an empty
  pipe, or any front within 15 min after the supply watch (detection
  supply.rs) saw supply return (FN-033); the live Monitor runs the watch
  at every sensor, and refills come out as REFILL warnings, none as
  bursts (V-S6). Supply cuts are alerts of their own since the same day
  (V-S9, FN-041). Still open: a burst DURING a refill is weighed against
  the refill (only sharpness and size speak for it); refilling
  inside the network solver (mixed flow, R-5 next rung); several pockets;
  real air-valve data. (Cavitation after the slam: done, V-A17.)
  Classes today: Burst, ValveClosure, PumpTrip, Unknown. Refilling after
  supply cuts is among the most violent SA transients
  (01_/the_operating_environment.txt section 2); it will come out as
  Unknown until a class and a simulation (S7 cavitation, air) exist.

G-19  Valves: response times unknown; PBV/GPV                    OPEN
  2026-10-01: valves may now MOVE in a transient (response time tau,
  V-A13); without tau they stay frozen. Still open: tau for real valves
  (files never give it; maker data or a recorded pressure step), and pilot
  dead time. 2026-09-30: pressure-reducing valves are real devices
  (V-A10), but in a transient a PRV is frozen at its steady opening. A real pilot-operated
  PRV opens over seconds to feed a burst below it [VERIFY response times
  from maker data]; frozen, it under-feeds the burst after the first
  seconds, and a PRV shut at the start stays shut.
  Same day, closed: routes through shut devices are cut and the share of
  a wave open devices pass enters placement and detectability (FN-029;
  valve_fronts.rs: PRV 0.25%, pump 0.02% vs the full simulator).
  Same day, closed: device states by the hour. A site may carry its
  Schedule (patterns, controls, power cuts; UTC offset); the monitor runs
  it for two days once, takes each hour's devices from the second, and
  locates, corroborates and sizes each event with those of its local hour
  (application/pipeline/monitor/hours.rs). Proved on a zone whose PRV is
  shut at night (tests/pipeline/simulation/valves_by_hour.rs): without the
  schedule a 02:00 burst heard by one logger was blamed on that logger
  ("a fault in S2"); with it, the alert says the valve is shut and the
  other logger's silence means nothing. Waiting and fusion still use the
  base state (they only wait a little longer than needed).
  Same day: PSV, FCV and TCV are devices too (V-A11), read from files;
  PBV and GPV (rare) remain plain connections. [RULES] read and run too
  (V-A12); premises on demands, fill/drain times and settings, and
  setting actions, are reported, not modelled.

G-20  Demands are fixed, whatever the pressure        IN PROGRESS
  2026-10-01: pressure-driven demand built (EPANET 2.2's law, exact by
  settling each junction FULL / PARTIAL / CUT; V-A15): steady and
  extended runs deliver less as pressure falls and warn SUPPLY SHORT /
  met again; files with DEMAND MODEL PDA run that way. Same day: an
  intake sump between canal and network (V-S5) - the town's supply falls
  away as the canal runs dry. Still open: pressure-driven demand in
  transients (the MOC keeps each junction's steady delivery).
  2026-10-01: the steady and extended-period solvers are demand-driven:
  a junction draws its demand even when the head there is too low to
  deliver it. Right while the network is supplied; wrong when it is not -
  a canal running dry (V-S5), a reservoir too low, a pump stopped. Today
  the runs SAY when this happens (supply lost, the canal's outlet dry);
  they do not reduce the draw. Fix: pressure-driven demand (a junction
  draws demand x f(pressure), Wagner's or EPANET 2.2's PDA [VERIFY the
  form]), needed anyway for draining and refilling (R-5, G-14).
