FN-001  2026-09-28  One burst raised twelve alerts (echoes)
  WHAT HAPPENED  In a small network the burst wave bounces off dead ends
    and junctions. Each reflection looked like a new event to a detector
    that re-armed after a fixed hold-off.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT In small commercial networks, echoes are the normal
    state for seconds after any event. "Re-arm after N seconds" is wrong.
  WHAT CHANGED   The detector waits until the signal settles, and listens
    through ringing with a raised floor (ring_units 1, settle band 4
    sigma). One burst, one alert. 04_/event_detection.txt section 4b.

FN-002  2026-09-28  A second burst during ringing was missed
  WHAT HAPPENED  "Wait until quiet" fixed FN-001 but went deaf: a second
    burst 3 s later was never reported.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Blindness is also a failure. Reduced sensitivity must be
    reported, never hidden (P6).
  WHAT CHANGED   Ringing floor instead of deafness; every stretch of
    reduced sensitivity is an Impairment span shown to the user.

FN-003  2026-09-28  A faulty sensor's jump came out as "Info"
  WHAT IT TAUGHT An event seen by one sensor only is not proof of
    nothing: the others may have been blind or the sensor may be broken.
  WHAT CHANGED   Corroboration: a single-sensor event is rated Review,
    with the reason in words ("silence means nothing" when others were
    impaired).

FN-004  2026-09-28  A sensor returning from a gap calibrated on echoes
  WHAT HAPPENED  After a power cut it learned its noise from ringing, so
    it came back too sensitive - or not sensitive enough.
  WHAT CHANGED   Calibration checks for ringing before trusting a noise
    estimate (tests/pipeline/simulation/live.rs, power cut).

FN-005  2026-09-28  Detection threshold measured, not assumed
  5 sigma caught and timed a front to 2 samples in 96% of 300 trials,
  6 sigma in 100% (tests/detection/analytical/sensitivity.rs). The
  placement advisor therefore plans with 6 sigma.

FN-006  2026-09-28  The best pair of loggers was not the two ends
  On the 3-sensor estate intuition said "put them at the two ends"; the
  scored answer was S1+S3. Lesson: placement is computed, not guessed -
  which is why the advisor exists.

FN-007  2026-09-28  Junctions, not kilometres, limit how far a burst is felt
  The first-front model matched full MOC within 0.05%. Each equal tee
  passes 2/3 of a front; friction takes about 3% per km. So a 1 l/s
  burst in 150 mm pipe survives about 7 tees but in theory ~95 km of
  straight pipe. See detection_range_and_scale.txt.

FN-008  2026-09-28  Placement analysis cost grows with length squared
  Campus (2.3 km): 86 s -> 54 s (debug) after caching the localiser per
  sensor set; about 6 s in release. A 10 km network would take ~20x
  longer. Gap G-10.

FN-009  2026-09-29  128 S/s read as 125 S/s from real-format timestamps
  WHAT HAPPENED  Logger files round times to the millisecond. At 128 S/s
  the true step is 7.8125 ms; rounded steps are 7 or 8 ms, and the MEDIAN
  step is 8 ms = 125 S/s. The whole recording would have been stretched.
  WHAT CHANGED   The reader uses the mean of ordinary steps and snaps to a
  whole rate only if every sample then fits its slot.

FN-010  2026-09-29  Clock time broke the locator's precision (critical)
  WHAT HAPPENED  Real files stamp seconds since 1970 (~1.79e9). Squared
  timing residuals at that size lose every significant digit: a burst's
  misfit came out as exactly 2^18 and its search stretch as 1166 m, rated
  Review instead of Alarm. All simulations started at t = 0, so no test
  had ever seen it.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Tests must use realistic absolute times, not just
  realistic signals. Near 1.79e9 s, 64-bit numbers are spaced 0.24 us
  apart - fine for storing a time, useless for squaring differences.
  WHAT CHANGED   Localiser and clock repair measure from the earliest
  arrival; invariant tests (tests/localisation/invariants/time_origin.rs)
  prove the answer does not depend on the time origin.

FN-013  2026-09-29  Scale study, first measurement (V-S4, `jk scale`)
  WHAT HAPPENED  Synthetic towns of 2, 10, 50, 100 km (crates/sim). The
    LIVE engine scaled well from the start (locating a burst: 66 ms at
    50 km, 230 ms at 100 km). The PLANNING side did not: scoring one
    logger placement took 112 s at 50 km; at 100 km it grew to 7 GB of
    memory and was stopped; choosing 50 loggers would have taken ~60 days.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Every localiser precomputed every candidate point of the
    whole network, and every placement tried rebuilt them all.
  WHAT CHANGED   See FN-014..016. Numbers: detection_range_and_scale.txt.

FN-014  2026-09-29  A loose bound skips nothing; shared routes are the key
  WHAT HAPPENED  First attempt at skipping hopeless pipes (branch and
    bound) pruned nothing: with 10% wave-speed doubt, a far pipe's
    predicted times are so vague that nothing looks impossible - if the
    doubt of every pipe on the way is counted in full.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Two waves travelling down the SAME pipes share that
    pipe's wave-speed error; it cancels when their arrival times are
    compared. Counting only the parts of the routes that differ makes far
    pipes clearly impossible. Plus: the determinant terms of the
    likelihood are largest with no wave-speed doubt at all (restricted-
    likelihood identity), which bounds them exactly.
  WHAT CHANGED   posterior/bound.rs; locating one burst at 50 km 66 -> 5 ms.
    locate_exhaustive kept to prove the answers identical (tests: 40
    random bursts, identical to the last digit).

FN-015  2026-09-29  A one-point stretch was silently skipped (caught)
  WHAT HAPPENED  Bounding stretches of 8 cells within a pipe: a stretch
    of ONE cell has zero width, and the bound skipped zero-width
    intervals - so it bounded nothing and the cell was dropped. The
    equality test caught it at once (90% stretch 259 m instead of 264 m).
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Every speed-up needs an exact twin to compare against.

FN-016  2026-09-29  Laptop timings wander by ~40%
  The same run took 72 s and 98 s an hour apart (heat, background work).
  Compare COUNTS (bursts located, points scored) - they do not wander -
  and treat seconds on this laptop as +/-40%.

FN-017  2026-09-29  A drifting clock's rate depends on whose seconds
  Estimating drift from each logger's own readings gives the rate per
  second OF THAT CLOCK: r / (1 + r) of the real-time rate r. 20 ppm vs
  19.9996 ppm - negligible, but a test demanding exactness found it. And
  clock time (1.79e9 s, stored to 0.24 us) limits a rate over 14 hours to
  ~1e-11 - the precision floor, stated in the test.

FN-018  2026-09-29  The plastic-wall simulator is first order in time
  V-A5 matched theory's frequency at once, but the decay came out 6% too
  fast at 200 reaches. Refining showed +12.7%, +6.4%, +3.2%: the error
  halves as the grid doubles, and extrapolating gives theory to 0.04%.
  So the physics is right and the scheme is first order; coarse grids
  over-damp plastic pipes a little. The test now demands the convergence
  itself, not one tolerance.

FN-019  2026-09-29  Zones: 50 km planning from hours to 13.5 minutes
  Planning a 50 km town zone by zone (10 zones of ~5 km, placement/zones.rs)
  chose 50 loggers in 13.5 min (whole-town search: stopped after 30+ min);
  mean 90% region 69 m vs 119 m for evenly spread loggers. The price,
  measured on a 5 km town: 92 m vs 83 m planning the whole town at once
  (+11%). Zones are the way to hundreds of km; faster zones next.

FN-020  2026-09-29  86% of whole-town planning was swaps; zones cost 3%
  WHAT HAPPENED  The owner would not accept losing 11% to zones. A timing
    probe on a 20 km town planned whole: choosing 20 loggers 168 s, then
    swaps 1031 s (10 180 swaps tried in 3 rounds) - each logger was offered
    every candidate whose bursts overlap its own, i.e. most of the town.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT A good swap moves a logger a little. Offering each logger
    only its 12 nearest candidates (by wave travel time): 20 km whole-town
    20 min -> 3.5 min, mean 90% region 60 m -> 61 m (+1.7%).
    Zones without polish: 42 s but 67 m (+10% - the owner's point).
    Zones then ONE whole-town polish (every logger tries its 12 nearest,
    scored on the whole town - it can cross zone borders): 2.8 min, 63 m
    (+3% vs whole-town), and 13% pinpointed vs 12%.
  WHAT CHANGED   advise.rs swap_rounds (12 nearest), zones.rs polish.
    50 km numbers: detection_range_and_scale.txt.

FN-021  2026-09-29  A sudden front shrank at HALF the right rate (fixed)
  WHAT HAPPENED  New test V-A6: a sharp front in a plastic pipe must shrink
    as exp(-kappa x), kappa = sum a c J / (g tau) - with the illustrative PE
    wall, half its height every ~104 m. The simulator shrank it at exactly
    half that rate, with a sawtooth behind it. V-A5 (a slow ring) had not
    shown it: slow waves are forgiving.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT At a front the wall has only just been loaded and creeps
    at its fastest (J sigma / tau). Taking the creep rate as an average over
    a time step AT A GRID POINT - as if the load rose gradually through the
    step - gives half of it, whatever the step. The creep must be integrated
    along the characteristic's own path (trapezoid: where the wave starts
    and where it arrives).
  WHAT CHANGED   pipe/viscoelastic.rs + moc/network/solver.rs. V-A6: kappa
    within 1.4%; V-A5 ring decay now +0.4% at 100 reaches (was +12.7%).

FN-022  2026-09-29  At 50 km, planning the whole town beats zones
  With near swaps (FN-020), 50 km, 50 loggers: whole town at once 30.7 min,
  mean 90% region 56 m; zones + polish 38.2 min, 60 m (+7%); evenly spread
  loggers 119 m. So `jk scale` now plans whole towns by default; zones are
  kept for networks too big to plan whole (hundreds of km).

FN-023  2026-09-30  Plastic pipes: the SHARP front fades, the FELT drop does not
  WHAT HAPPENED  After adding plastic damping to detectability (a front
    shrinking exp(-kappa x)) the report said a burst at S2 was felt 20x less
    than in rigid pipe. Checking in the simulator: behind the shrinking
    front the creeping wall builds the drop back up - within 0.25 s S2 felt
    76% of the rigid drop (illustrative wall), 75% with the measured HDPE.
  WHAT IT TAUGHT Two different questions. TIMING (where the burst is) uses
    the sharp front: it fades as exp(-kappa x). NOTICING (is there a burst)
    uses the drop over a detection window: plastic softens it a little
    (relax) and barely damps it. Mixing them up would have made plastic
    networks look nearly blind.
  WHAT CHANGED   Wall window_response (theory at omega = 1/W); detectability
    and the advisor use the felt drop (PlacementOptions::window, 0.25 s);
    first_fronts keeps the sharp front; felt_heads is new. Checked on a
    plastic estate, both walls: every reading within 1-6% of the simulator.

FN-025  2026-09-30  A baseline estimated from few nights raises false alarms
  The night watch (slow leaks, jk-balance) with Page's classic CUSUM
  (drift 0.5, alarm 5) on a 14-night baseline gave a false alarm every
  ~270 nights per zone on leak-free simulated zones - the theory for known
  mean and spread promises ~930. The baseline's own uncertainty adds to
  the noise. Measured grid: 28-night baseline (four whole weeks) and alarm
  at 6 -> one per ~590 nights (1.6 years), still catching a 1.2-spread
  leak within a week 72% of the time. Defaults set from the measurement.

FN-026  2026-09-30  A sparse steady solver: 150 km from 30 s to 7 ms
  The steady solver stored its nodal matrix dense (n^2, n^3 work): ~2000
  junctions (~180 km) was the limit and 150 km took 29.6 s. The matrix has
  one row per junction but only a few non-zeros per row (the pipes).
  jk_kernel::sparse: reverse Cuthill-McKee ordering + envelope Cholesky,
  pattern set up once, refilled every Newton round; checked against the
  dense solver on random networks. `jk scale --no-simulation`: steady state
  100 km 6 ms, 150 km 7 ms, 250 km 15 ms, 500 km 55 ms, 1000 km (10 813
  junctions) 0.29 s; locating a burst at 1000 km 0.1 s.

FN-027  2026-09-30  Wave simulation on four cores: 150 km from ~22 to 4.8 s
  Within a time step each pipe's interior depends only on the old step, so
  on grids of 50 000+ points the pipes are shared among up to four threads
  (contiguous runs of about equal points); nodes stay on one thread. Same
  arithmetic, bit for bit (unit test). 150 km: 4.8 s per simulated second
  (was ~22); 50 km barely gains (0.8 s) - a step is short and starting
  threads each step costs most of the saving; a persistent thread pool
  would recover it if needed.

FN-028  2026-09-30  Extended runs smeared each hour's demand into the next
  Heun's method takes the slope at both ends of a step. At the last step
  of each hour the end slope was solved with the NEXT hour's demand, so
  a jump in the pattern (e.g. 0.4 -> 1.5 at 06:00) leaked half a step
  early. Found while adding controls, which need steps that land exactly
  on switch times. Now steps never cross a pattern edge and both slopes
  use the demand in force during the step. Same fix makes a pump switched
  at a tank level start at that level, not up to a step later (V-A9).

FN-029  2026-09-30  A PRV is a partial mirror for pressure waves
  Frozen at its steady opening (it cannot move in the time a wave takes
  to pass), a PRV passing flow is an orifice: a wave dH arriving from
  upstream passes on as 2 B dH / (2 B + 2 R Q0). For a valve dropping 30 m
  at 50 l/s between DN300 pipes (B = 1443 s/m^2, 2 R Q0 = 1200 s/m^2),
  71% passes and 29% reflects (V-A10, matched to 6e-10). A shut PRV (zone
  held from elsewhere) reflects everything. Consequences: (1) loggers on
  the far side of a PRV hear a burst weaker - placement and detectability
  should count it; (2) the localiser's routes through a shut PRV or check
  valve are wrong - such routes should be cut, as routes through
  reservoirs already are (gap G-19).
  Acted on the same day: graph/devices.rs cuts routes through shut
  devices and gives placement/detectability each device's share,
  T = 2 Bout / (Bin + Bout + r) with r from the steady state (a PRV:
  2 dH0 / Q0; a running pump: its curve's droop - 86% passed a pump
  delivering 150 l/s). Full simulator vs quick model: PRV 0.25%, pump
  0.02%, shut PRV nothing either way (valve_fronts.rs). Also found: the
  steady solver could not settle an active PRV driven backwards through
  frictionless pipes (no convergence before its state was checked); it
  now shuts it and retries, as it does for pumps.

FN-030  2026-10-01  A PRV that moves overshoots its setting after a burst
  With a response time (V-A13), a PRV restores its setting after a burst
  below it - but not smoothly: it opens wide while the burst's low-pressure
  wave rings through, then lets too much through before it closes again.
  In V-A13 (25 mm hole 500 m below a PRV holding 50 m, mean head just below
  the valve over 9-13 s): tau 3 s -> 54.8 m, tau 10 s -> 64.7 m; both settle
  on 50.000 m later. Frozen, the head stays at 14 m. So after a burst the
  zone below a slow PRV can see pressure ABOVE normal for tens of seconds -
  a second load on weakened pipes, and a pattern the classifier should not
  read as a new event. Model-based: [VERIFY] against a real valve's
  recorded response before it is used to advise anyone (P4).

FN-031  2026-10-01  Two solvers must step together, not swap histories
  First coupling of canal and network through an intake sump: each solver
  ran the whole 6-8 h, then handed the other its history (levels one way,
  flows the other), repeated until they agreed. It did not settle - not
  even halving each turn. Reason: the interface is stiff. A 100 m^2 sump
  behind an inlet passing 1.0 sqrt(dh) reacts in ~60 s, and near drying
  the inlet's flow c sqrt(depth) has an infinite slope; an error of 1 cm in
  one turn grows to metres over a long window. Fix: step them together -
  the canal as a live source the network asks every step (level), then
  draws from over the step (flow, capped by what it holds), the stiff
  inlet solved inside the network's step with steps no longer than the
  sump's time constant. One pass, 0.1 s, water exact at the interface
  (V-S5). Lesson for every future coupling (sources, mixed flow): couple
  in time, not over whole runs.

FN-032  2026-10-01  A half-sized air valve is worse than none
  First refilling runs (V-A16; 500 m DN200, 30 m supply, f = 0.02): with
  the air sealed in, it cushions the column - peak 33 m. With no air at
  all the column slams the closed end at 3.4 m/s - 350 m. Between them
  the air valve decides: up to 10 mm the cushion holds (31-33 m); at
  20 mm the air leaves fast enough to let the column run but not fast
  enough to stop it slamming, 169 m; from 40 mm on it is a full slam
  (341-350 m). So an air valve that is merely "there" says nothing about
  safety - its size against the fill rate does, and a fast refill through
  an open valve is the worst case (agrees with Zhou, Hicks & Steffler
  2002). Second lesson: the air's polytropic exponent moves a sealed
  pocket's peak by 2x (205 m adiabatic vs 458 m isothermal, frictionless)
  [VERIFY which applies to field refills; the literature uses 1.2-1.4].
  Consequence: any refill advice must be simulated with the actual valve
  size and a range of exponents (P4).
