INVERSE TRANSIENT ANALYSIS (ITA) AND PROACTIVE PROBING
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

1. The idea
-----------
Forward problem: given network parameters theta (wave speeds, friction,
leak locations and sizes, device states), the MOC model predicts the
pressure response y(theta) at the sensors to a transient.
Inverse problem: given measured y_obs, find theta.
      theta_hat = argmin_theta  || y_obs - y(theta) ||^2_W  + regulariser
(Liggett & Chen 1994 introduced ITA for leak detection and calibration in
networks; Vitkovsky et al. 2000 with genetic algorithms.)

2. Why it is hard
-----------------
- Ill-posed: many parameter combinations fit similar data; leaks, unknown
  wave speeds, friction and device behaviour trade off against each other.
- Non-convex: many local minima (reflections align in multiple ways).
- Model error: unmodelled features (air pockets, unknown connections,
  viscoelasticity) masquerade as leaks.
- Cost: each objective evaluation is a full transient simulation.

3. How we intend to make it tractable
-------------------------------------
- Bayesian formulation: posterior p(theta | y_obs), not a single best fit.
  MCMC / sequential Monte Carlo over a reduced parameter set, reporting
  credible regions (principle P3).
- Gradients: differentiable MOC solver or adjoint method -> gradient-based
  optimisation and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo instead of blind search.
- Surrogates: neural or reduced-order surrogates of the forward model,
  trained on simulations, used inside the sampler; verified against the
  full MOC for final answers.
- Sparsity: leaks are few; use sparsity-promoting priors (e.g. horseshoe /
  L1) on leak sizes over candidate locations.
- Staging: calibrate wave speeds and friction on events with KNOWN
  sources first; only then search for unknown leaks.

4. Proactive probing (active sensing)
-------------------------------------
Rather than waiting for accidental events, generate small, SAFE, known
transients deliberately (e.g. a controlled fast-acting valve on a small
bleed/hydrant, a few l/s for a fraction of a second, producing a surge of
~0.1-0.5 bar - far below any damage threshold) and read the network's
response - like sonar or seismic surveying.
Uses:
  - wave speed calibration per pipe,
  - detection of existing leaks from their reflections,
  - pipe condition assessment (wall thinning -> lower local wave speed;
    blockages/partially closed valves -> reflections),
  - locating unknown features (unrecorded valves, closed boundary valves).
Frequency-domain variants (frequency response function from a pseudo-
random binary excitation) give better signal-to-noise for the same small
amplitude (Lee et al. 2005). This is a strong candidate for a signature
capability of the engine. Safety checks per principle P4 apply.
