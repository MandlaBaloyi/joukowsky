P3 - LOCATING THE SOURCE OF A TRANSIENT
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

1. Single pipe, two sensors (the basic idea)
--------------------------------------------
Sensors at both ends of a pipe of length L; burst at distance x from
sensor 1. The wave reaches sensor 1 after x/a and sensor 2 after (L-x)/a:
      dt = t1 - t2 = (2x - L) / a      =>      x = (L + a * dt) / 2
Error propagation:
      dx = (1/2) * sqrt( (a * sigma_dt)^2 + (dt * sigma_a)^2 )
  - timing error term: a*sigma_dt/2  (why clocks matter, 03_)
  - wave speed error term: dt*sigma_a/2 (why learning a matters, 02_)
    Zero when the burst is at the midpoint, largest near the sensors.
Published negative-pressure-wave studies on pipelines report errors of a
few % of sensor spacing [see references]; our target of <= 2% of pipe
length on an instrumented pipe is consistent with that.

2. Networks: arrival times on a graph
-------------------------------------
In a branched/looped network the first arrival travels along the
fastest path. Define edge travel time tau_e = L_e / a_e. For each sensor
s, run Dijkstra from s over the graph with weights tau_e, giving travel
time T_s(n) to every node n. A candidate source is a point on edge
(u,v) at fraction lambda in [0,1]; its travel time to s is:
      T_s(u,v,lambda) = min( T_s(u) + lambda * tau_uv,
                             T_s(v) + (1 - lambda) * tau_uv )
Observed arrival times t_s = t0 + T_s(x0) + e_s, with e_s ~ N(0, sigma_s^2)
(sigma_s includes clock uncertainty and onset-picking uncertainty).
The unknown emission time t0 is eliminated analytically (it is the
weighted mean of t_s - T_s(x0)). Then the posterior over the source:
      p(x0 | t) ~ prior(x0) * exp( - sum_s (t_s - t0_hat - T_s(x0))^2
                                     / (2 sigma_s^2) )
evaluated on a fine grid along every edge. The prior encodes pipe
failure likelihood (age, material, history) - this is where the risk
model (P5) feeds back into localisation.
Wave speed uncertainty is handled by marginalising over samples of the
a_e posterior (Monte Carlo), which widens the location posterior honestly.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE (2026-09-28, crates/domain/localisation): the Monte
Carlo version was built and FAILED its calibration test - the 90% region
held the truth in only 8% of trials. Cause: importance-sampling
degeneracy. With 3+ sensors the data constrain the wave speeds, one
sampled set fits far better than the rest, and the average collapses onto
it. Replaced by an exact linearised treatment: each pipe's relative
wave-speed error (sigma_a) becomes a travel-time error along every route
that uses it, so the arrival errors are CORRELATED,
    C_st = sigma_s^2 delta_st + sigma_a^2 sum_pipes w_sp w_tp
(w_sp = travel time of sensor s's wave on pipe p), and each candidate is
scored by generalised least squares with t0 marginalised, including the
log det C term. Deterministic, no sampling. Calibration over 400 random
bursts with 3% speed errors: 92.8% inside the 90% region.
Also implemented: first arrivals never pass THROUGH a reservoir (it
reflects, R = -1); a timing-misfit output flags arrivals that no single
source explains (two events, a wrong wave speed, a bad clock).

Output: ranked list of pipe segments with probability mass; a 90%
credible region drawn on the map.

3. Beyond first arrivals
------------------------
- Amplitude: the first-arrival amplitude decays through junctions
  (T = 2/3 per tee) and with damping; comparing amplitudes across sensors
  adds information independent of timing.
- Full waveform matching: simulate the event from each candidate location
  with MOC and compare whole waveforms (the likelihood in section 2
  becomes a waveform misfit). Expensive but far more informative in
  networks with many reflections. Use a surrogate model or precomputed
  Green's-function library (linearised response of each sensor to a unit
  flow step at each candidate location) to make this fast.
- Existing sensors of opportunity: bursts also show up as flow increases
  at district/site meters; fuse with pressure data.

4. Sensor placement (a design problem we must solve early)
----------------------------------------------------------
Choose S sensor locations to maximise expected localisation performance
over the distribution of possible bursts (weighted by pipe risk), subject
to install constraints given by whoever installs them (existing
chambers, power, GNSS sky view).
Objective candidates: expected posterior entropy of x0; expected size of
90% credible region; worst-case ambiguity. Greedy selection with
submodularity arguments is a reasonable start; mixed-integer or
evolutionary methods for refinement. Delivered as a software placement
advisor: we tell the installer (a partner, not us) where sensors should
go.

Implementation note: the placement advisor (2026-09-28)
-------------------------------------------------------
crates/domain/localisation/src/placement/ {coverage.rs, advise.rs};
tests/localisation/analytical/placement.rs.

Score of a placement: bursts every 20 m along every pipe; for each, the
exact arrival times at the sensors, timing sigma 1 ms [VERIFY on field
data], book wave speeds doubted by 10% (one sigma, independently per
pipe), located by the real localiser (5 m candidates). Recorded: the 90%
region length. Objective = its mean over the network (weighted by length,
optionally risk) = the expected search length for a repair crew. Also
reported: share of pipe length PINPOINTED (region <= 20 m) and per-pipe
figures. Choice: best pair (exhaustive up to 300 pairs, else the pair
furthest apart), greedy additions (each step recorded: "what each extra
logger buys"), then swaps until none helps.

Speed: a Localiser prepares everything that does not depend on the
arrival times once per sensor set (routes, cells, predicted travel times,
wave-speed covariance); each trial burst then costs one small Cholesky
per cell. Bit-identical to the earlier localise (every number printed by
the test suite unchanged). Campus (2.31 km, 11 candidates, 6 loggers):
86 s -> 54 s on the dev laptop.

Findings:
  1. Geometry: only bursts BETWEEN sensors are pinpointed; a branch
     beyond the outermost sensor looks like its junction. Estate, loggers
     at S2 + S3, exact wave speeds: T-S2 and T-S3 100% pinpointed (region
     10 m); the 700 m upstream of T blind (region 635 m = 90% of 700 m +
     one cell). No logger can sit beyond a reservoir: the supply main
     stays blind, and the report says so.
  2. The objective is not "loggers at the ends". Best pair on the estate:
     S1 + S3 (two blind zones of 400 and 500 m: expected region 336 m),
     not S2 + S3 (one blind zone of 700 m: 413 m).
  3. Wave-speed doubt dominates. Estate, 3 loggers: pinpointed 78% with
     exact or 1% wave speeds, 69% at 2%, 0% at 5% and beyond (regions
     60-100 m). Calibrating wave speeds from known events (F9) to about
     2% is worth as much as extra loggers - a concrete target.
  4. Campus (ring + plastic branches), 6 loggers: greedy 2 -> 6 loggers
     cut the mean region 291 -> 89 m; swaps moved the ring junctions to
     building ends: 77 m, 22% pinpointed. Branches with a logger at their
     end ~20 m; the ring 60-70 m; the unlogged 90 m branch and the
     municipal connection blind (named in the report).
Not yet: detectability of small bursts at the chosen sensors (V-S2);
robustness over a posterior of wave speeds (F10 proper); speed for large
networks (cost grows with length squared: a 10 km network would take
~20x longer - options: coarser trial bursts far from sensors, parallel
scoring, or an analytical score for the pair search).
