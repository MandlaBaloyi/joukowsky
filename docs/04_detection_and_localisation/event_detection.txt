P1 - EVENT DETECTION
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

1. Signal model
---------------
At sensor s:
    p(t) = b(t) + sum_j w_j(t - t_j) + n(t)
  b(t)  slow baseline: diurnal demand, reservoir level, PRV behaviour
        (minutes-hours)
  w_j   transient waveforms starting at unknown times t_j
        (steps, spikes, damped oscillations; ms-s)
  n(t)  noise: sensor/electrical noise + hydraulic "noise" from turbulence
        and small demand changes (taps, toilets, irrigation valves)
Detection = deciding online that some t_j has occurred, and estimating t_j
precisely (the arrival time feeds localisation).

2. A layered detector (edge -> cloud)
-------------------------------------
Layer 0  Pre-processing (edge)
         Anti-alias filtering, decimation to surveillance rate, sensor
         health checks (stuck value, saturation, spikes > physical limits).
Layer 1  Baseline tracking (edge)
         Robust estimate of b(t) (e.g. median / exponential smoothing /
         small Kalman filter). Residual r(t) = p(t) - b_hat(t).
Layer 2  Change detection on the residual (edge) - candidates:
  (a) Two-sided CUSUM (Page 1954) on residual increments:
          S+_k = max(0, S+_{k-1} + r'_k - nu)
          S-_k = max(0, S-_{k-1} - r'_k - nu)
          alarm if S+ or S- > h
      nu (drift) and h (threshold) set the trade-off between detection
      delay and false-alarm rate (average run length ARL0). Cheap; ideal
      for an MCU.
  (b) GLR test for a step of unknown size: maximise the likelihood ratio
      over change time within a sliding window; more powerful, more
      compute.
  (c) Multi-scale wavelet detail energy (Haar/Daubechies): a step appears
      as aligned large coefficients across scales; noise does not.
      Used successfully for burst detection in instrumented networks
      (e.g. WaterWiSe Singapore, Srirangarajan et al. 2013).
  (d) Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams & MacKay 2007):
      posterior over "time since last change"; natural confidence output.
  Plan: implement (a), (c), (d) on recorded + simulated data; compare by
  ROC curves at fixed detection delay (07_validation). Pick the best
  performer that fits the edge compute budget; run heavier ones in cloud.
Layer 3  Arrival-time refinement (edge on capture-rate data, or cloud)
         Precise onset estimate: fit a local model (e.g. piecewise-linear
         or AIC-based onset picker, borrowed from seismology) to the
         full-rate buffer around the alarm; report onset time with
         uncertainty sigma_t.
Layer 4  Network-level fusion (cloud)
         An event is confirmed when arrivals at multiple sensors are
         physically consistent: time differences <= travel times along
         the network (a coincidence gate derived from the graph and wave
         speeds). Physically impossible combinations = separate events or
         a sensor fault.

3. False alarms are the real enemy
----------------------------------
A system that raises ten false bursts a week will be switched off. So:
  - thresholds are set per sensor from its own noise statistics
    (learned during a calibration period),
  - known operational events (pump schedules, load-shedding times,
    operator-logged valve work) are fed in as context and down-weighted
    or auto-labelled,
  - alerts go out only after Layer 4 fusion + classification (P2) with
    confidence above a threshold; lower-confidence events are logged.

4. Implementation note: after an event - echoes and ringing (2026-09-28)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built in crates/domain/detection/src/detector.rs; measured in
tests/pipeline/simulation/scenarios.rs.

Problem. After an event the network RINGS: the wave echoes off
reservoirs, tees and dead ends, and at a sensor each echo is a real small
pressure step. The first pipeline re-armed each detector after a fixed
1 s hold-off; ONE simulated burst on the test estate then produced 12
alerts, including a second, false Alarm.

Tried and rejected: stay blind until the record is quiet again. It fixed
the single burst, but a flowing network (quasi-steady friction) still
rang at 0.3-0.9 m 27 s later, and a second burst at t = 30 s was missed.
Silence while blind is not "no events" (P6).

What is built:
  a. After an event the sensor is blind for max(hold-off, settle window).
     The pipeline sets the settle window to at least one echo round trip
     of the network: 2 x (longest fastest travel time between any two
     nodes).
  b. It then listens at REDUCED sensitivity while the network rings: the
     noise unit ("floor") becomes max(sigma, S / ring_units), S = largest
     deviation from the median over the last settle window,
     ring_units = 1 (so ringing sits at 1 unit, below the CUSUM drift
     k = 3; a new event must be ~3x the ringing). The forecast is
     re-centred on that median. The floor is re-measured every quarter
     window and may fall by at most half per window (ringing beats).
  c. A swing with no step behind it (CUSUM alarm, no onset found) raises
     the floor and listening continues - no blind period.
  d. Full sensitivity returns when the window looks like plain noise:
     S <= 4 sigma. (Gaussian noise over n samples peaks near
     sqrt(2 ln n) sigma = 3.4 sigma for n = 300.) A looser band (10 sigma
     was tried) let residual echoes of 6-20 cm re-trigger the
     full-sensitivity CUSUM: one false single-sensor alert.
  e. Honesty (P2): every event records how its sensor re-armed
     (Settled / Ringing{floor} / RecordEnded), and the detector reports
     the spans when the sensor was blind or at reduced sensitivity. Only
     outside those spans may a sensor's silence count as evidence that
     nothing happened.

Result on the estate (128 S/s, 2 cm noise): one burst -> exactly one
Alarm; two bursts 27 s apart on a flowing network -> exactly two Alarms,
each seen by all three sensors, located within 1 m.
Cost: while ringing, events smaller than ~3x the ringing go unseen - and
the spans say so. A model that PREDICTS the echoes (subtract the
simulated ringing, then detect on the residual) would remove most of this
cost; it needs a calibrated network model (twin, 05_) - open.

4b. Measured sensitivity (2026-09-28)
------------------------------------
tests/detection/analytical/sensitivity.rs: a sudden drop hidden in
Gaussian noise at a random moment within a sample, 300 trials per size;
"caught" = an event timed within 2 samples of the truth:
  3 sigma 1.7%   4 sigma 61%   5 sigma 96%   6 sigma 100%   8+ 100%
The placement advisor treats a front of >= 6 sigma as felt.

5. Implementation note: live detection (2026-09-28)
---------------------------------------------------
crates/domain/detection/src/detector/ {mod.rs, floor.rs, stream.rs}.

The chain runs LIVE (DetectorStream: samples in pieces of any size) and
the whole-record run is the live one fed everything at once. The same
steps, written as a state machine - calibrating, watching, picking a
front, blind - that pauses whenever it needs samples not yet received
(picking needs `after` = 6 samples past the alarm; re-arming needs the
settle window). Tested: pieces of 1, 7, 128 and random sizes give exactly
the events and spans of the whole record; every number printed by the
whole test suite was unchanged by the rewrite.

  - Memory is bounded: only record a later step can still need is kept,
    at most `memory` (60 s) plus a settle window. A change building up for
    longer than `memory` is not picked as a front (a slow ramp is for the
    hours-scale methods, rule E3).
  - Watermark: the time before which no future event can have arrived
    (while CUSUM evidence builds up it waits at where the change began).
    Fusion uses it to know when a sensor's silence is final.
  - Impaired stretches are typed: Calibrating, Blind (after its own
    event), Ringing{floor} (reduced sensitivity, with the highest floor).
  - One stream covers one continuous stretch. After a gap (telemetry
    context) a new stream calibrates afresh. If its calibration window is
    not quiet - the sensor came back while the network rang - it starts at
    reduced sensitivity, exactly as after an event of its own. Found by a
    test: without this, a sensor back from a 3.5 s power cut would take
    every echo for an event.

6. Supply states: empty pipes and returning supply (2026-10-01)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The front detector looks for waves and lets slow ramps pass as drift
(DetectorConfig.memory). A pipe refilling against trapped air is such a
ramp - the air squeezed over a minute or more - so its start is never a
front (FN-033). What marks it is the state before: a drained pipe reads
about 0 (air at atmospheric pressure; below 0 in a vacuum) for minutes
to hours. The supply watch (crates/domain/detection/src/supply.rs,
empty_spells) finds spells at or below 2 m lasting >= 60 s - a pump
trip's down-surge touches the vapour floor for seconds, which is a
transient - bridging wanders above 2 m shorter than 2 s, and reports when
each spell ended: the supply returned. A spell running into the start or
end of the record says so (began before / not yet ended, P6). The
classifier takes the returns as context: fronts within 15 min after one
belong to the refill. [VERIFY the 2 m / 60 s / 15 min on field records
of supply cuts.] Not yet run by the live Monitor (G-14).
