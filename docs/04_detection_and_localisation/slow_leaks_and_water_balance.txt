SLOW LEAKS AND WATER BALANCE
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 (2026-09-30) - step R-3 of 02_physics_of_transients/
sources_dams_rivers_canals.txt; gap G-12

Why
---
A leak that grows over days sends no wave: the transient engine cannot
see it (P2). But it takes water, and water is counted. Where a zone is
bounded by flow meters - a district metered area (DMA), a bulk main
between two meters, a site behind its bulk meter - the balance

  used(t) = sum over meters of (+Q_in or -Q_out) - dV/dt (storage)

is everything used inside: consumption plus leakage. At night (about
02:00-04:00 local) consumption is at its lowest and most regular; the
MINIMUM NIGHT FLOW (MNF) is then mostly leakage. A new leak is a lasting
step up in the MNF, night after night.

Terms (ubiquitous language)
---------------------------
  zone          an area bounded by flow meters (inflows +, outflows -)
  balance       used(t) above, per reading
  night flow    the mean balance over the night window, one per night;
                a night with less than half its readings is MISSING, not
                filled (P6)
  leak alarm    a lasting rise in night flow beyond what nights vary by

Method
------
1. Balance per reading; storage change from tank volumes if given.
2. Night flow per night (local window, default 02:00-04:00).
3. Baseline from the first nights (default 28 - four whole weeks): their
   mean mu and the
   night-to-night spread s (which already contains meter noise and the
   natural variation of night use).
4. One-sided CUSUM on z_k = (night_k - mu) / s, drift 0.5, alarm at 6: a
   step of d spreads is caught after about 6 / (d - 0.5) nights. Chosen
   by simulation (FN-025): Page's classic 5 with a 14-night baseline gave
   a false alarm every ~270 nights per zone - the baseline's own
   uncertainty adds to the noise; 28 nights and 6: one per ~590 nights.
5. The alarm names the first night of the rise (where the CUSUM last
   left zero) and the leak's size: the mean night flow since then minus
   mu, with its uncertainty.

Revised on real data (2026-10-05, FN-048; crates/domain/balance/src/
leak.rs). L-Town's two years (BattLeDIM, 33 true leaks) broke the method
above: 3 leaks caught within a week, and blind for 341 days after an
alarm, because the new 28-night baseline held the leak, its repair and
the season. Now:
  a. WEEKLY PROFILE: if the baseline's weekday means differ by more than
     chance (F test, 1%, needs 28 nights), each weekday gets its own
     expected level (L-Town: weekend nights 10-25 m3/h above weeknights;
     the spread fell from 11.3 to 6.0 m3/h).
  b. SEASONS: while nothing builds up, each weekday's expected level
     follows its own nights over four occurrences (follow_nights 28 / 7).
  c. TWO-SIDED: a lasting FALL - a repair, or less use - is reported too
     and steps the level down; on L-Town the falls match the true repair
     dates within 1-3 days.
  d. STEP AND SETTLE: after an alarm or a fall the level steps by the
     size found and is refined for 14 nights as the MEDIAN of the nights
     since the change began (a size measured because it crossed the
     alarm line is biased high; stepping by it alone ping-ponged between
     alarms and falls - one false alarm per 250 nights in simulation; the
     median, not the mean, so a second leak starting inside the window is
     not swallowed). No new alarm in those 14 nights - but at their end a
     clear split of the window's own nights into two levels is reported
     as a second change, and the level moved to it (FN-051).
  f. HOLIDAYS: the site's calendar marks the night opening each holiday
     and the night after; they are not judged (mark_holidays, G-24).
  e. ROBUST: one night counts at most 3 spreads (z clipped): leak-free
     nights are heavy-tailed (typical spread 2-3 m3/h about their own
     weekday, but the odd night - often a holiday - 20-30 m3/h off).
Result, L-Town 2018-2019 (tests/balance/datasets/ltown_leaks.rs),
scored one to one - each alarm matched to the newest leak running when
it is raised, each leak to one alarm (FN-051; an earlier, kinder rule
reported "25 of 33"): 21 of 33 leaks caught within a month (14 of the 18
reaching 15 m3/h), 28 at all, 9 within a week; 18 repairs seen; longest
silence while a big leak came and went 63 days (was 341). Simulation unchanged: one false alarm per ~560
nights, a leak at the d_min arithmetic caught within a week 75% of the
time.
Still missed: one town-wide balance cannot see leaks under about 8
m3/h within a week, nor a leak that begins as another of its size is
repaired - smaller zones (district metered areas) can; a leak that
begins in the first nights after an alarm is taken into the new level.

What meters can and cannot see (P2, P3)
---------------------------------------
A meter's CALIBRATION error is a constant bias: it shifts every night
alike, so it does not hide a change - it only makes the absolute leakage
level uncertain. What hides a change is the night-to-night SPREAD s. The
smallest leak caught within n nights is about

  d_min = s (0.5 + 6 / n)            (1.36 s within a week)

and the engine states it per zone. Example: a bulk main carrying 300 l/s
by day but 60 l/s at night, with meters varying 0.5% night to night:
s ~ 0.3 l/s, so leaks of ~0.4 l/s show within a week - leaks far smaller
than any wave could reveal on a 1 m pipe.

Validation
----------
tests/balance/: analytical (balance and night flows exact on constructed
series; the detectable-size formula), simulation (90 synthetic days with
a diurnal pattern, night-to-night variation and meter noise: no alarm
without a leak; a leak of 3 spreads caught within days, first night and
size right within their uncertainty).
