THE SCALE OF NON-REVENUE WATER (NRW) IN SOUTH AFRICA
STATUS: DRAFT v0.1 - for review

The system is not failing slowly; it is actively haemorrhaging. The data
validating the demand for a private-sector telemetry solution is staggering.

According to the official No Drop report and current statistics:

  * Approximately 47% of all treated municipal tap water in South Africa is
    lost as Non-Revenue Water (NRW) before it can be billed.
  * At least 20% of this total loss is due to direct physical leaks and
    burst pipes. [SEE NOTE 3 - the evidence suggests the physical share is
    considerably larger than this]
  * The situation is deteriorating unevenly across provinces. For example,
    KwaZulu-Natal loses roughly 60.5% of its water, while Limpopo loses
    nearly 58%.

The government cannot fund new infrastructure to replace the leaks. By
targeting commercial clients (estates, factories, universities) who suffer
from these localised failures, the joukowsky engine bypasses the broken
municipal supply chain. It provides commercial clients the predictive
intelligence necessary to isolate internal leaks and shut off systems before
a municipal pressure surge destroys their internal piping.

RESEARCH NOTES (added during planning - for review)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Definitions (IWA standard water balance) - we must use these precisely.
   System Input Volume (SIV)
     = Authorised consumption + Water losses
   Water losses
     = Real losses (physical: leaks, bursts, reservoir overflows)
     + Apparent losses (meter under-registration, theft/illegal
       connections, data and billing errors)
   Non-Revenue Water (NRW)
     = Unbilled authorised consumption + Water losses
   So NRW > water losses > real losses. joukowsky directly attacks REAL
   losses (and, via surge monitoring, the damage that creates them).
   It does not fix billing or theft.

2. Headline numbers found so far (DWS No Drop reports):
     National NRW, 2014 ........................ ~37%
     National NRW, 2023 No Drop ................. 47.4%
     National water losses, 2023 ................ 40.8% of SIV
     System Input Volume, 2022/23 ............... ~4.39 billion m^3/yr
     National NRW, 2025 No Drop (rel. 31 Mar 2026)  47.3%
     International benchmark NRW (often quoted) . ~30%
     Per-capita consumption (national avg) ...... ~218 l/c/d vs ~173 intl
   Provincial NRW, 2023:
     KwaZulu-Natal 60.5% | Free State 59.5% | Limpopo 57.7% |
     Gauteng 49.2% | Western Cape 27.6% (lowest)
   [VERIFY all against the primary PDFs: ND_2023_Report.pdf and the 2025
   No Drop report - these were collected from secondary reporting because
   the DWS server was not reachable from the research environment.]

3. On "at least 20% physical".
   The 2023 figure of 40.8% water losses includes apparent losses.
   Secondary reporting puts physical leakage at roughly a third of SIV
   (~32%), i.e. about two-thirds of NRW [VERIFY - source trail not yet
   confirmed]. The "at least 20%" statement in the text above is therefore
   conservative and defensible; if the ~32% figure is confirmed we should
   use it instead, stating clearly whether it is % of SIV or % of NRW.

4. What 47% means in physical terms (our working, to be checked):
     4.39e9 m^3/yr * 0.408 water losses ~ 1.79e9 m^3/yr lost
     ~ 4.9 million m^3 per day
     ~ 57 m^3 every second, around the clock.
   If real losses are ~32% of SIV: ~1.4e9 m^3/yr physically leaking.

5. Trend matters more than level.
   NRW went from ~37% (2014) to ~47% (2023) and did not improve by 2025.
   This is consistent with ageing infrastructure, deferred maintenance and
   operating practices that damage pipes (surges, intermittent supply) -
   the failure mode this engine is designed to observe.
